* **DEC-0011 (2026-07-25) — B7 seed corpus is synthetic, with a named open-licence subset (closed, July 2026).** Discharges the B7 entry criterion requiring a corpus sourcing decision before any ingest (RAT-01 §5, B7). The B7 RAG corpus will not contain FCA Handbook, JMLSG, UK Finance or other restrictively licensed third-party text, in the repo or in any local vector index. Basis: the FCA Handbook terms prohibit including site content in any public **or private** electronic retrieval system or service without prior written permission, require a licence agreement for reproduction, and separately prohibit commercial use without a licence; permitted use is limited to printing and downloading extracts for personal use or use within an individual firm. A ChromaDB index is a private electronic retrieval system on the plain reading. JMLSG terms permit download for personal or legitimate internal business purposes but prohibit distribution to third parties, circulation for profit, and transmission to any other person; a client demonstration surfacing retrieved chunks is not internal business use. Rejected — local-only ingest outside the repo: keeping third-party text out of git addresses publication but not the prohibition on inclusion in a private retrieval system, because the local index is itself the restricted artefact. Recorded as considered and rejected because it is the intuitive answer and will be proposed again. Adopted: (a) a synthetic seed corpus of 50+ documents authored for the build, mirroring the structure and register of UK fraud and financial-crime guidance without reproducing any of it; (b) a small named subset of genuinely open material — sources under the Open Government Licence, including FCA outputs where the OGL is expressly stated to apply, legislation.gov.uk, and HM Treasury published material — to prove the ingest path handles real documents with real structure. The corpus manifest licence column therefore carries two values only: `synthetic-arcaai` and `OGL-v3.0`. Consequences: ground truth is controlled, so the RAGAS eval set and the B7 negative test are constructible rather than found; and the B7 entry criterion moves from externally dependent to self-resolvable (RAT-01 §3.2), removing the longest pole from the B7 entry path. Forward position: licensed regulatory content in a client deployment is either covered by the client’s own permissions as a regulated firm, or requires a licence negotiated by ArcaAI at commercialisation — a commercial/B12 item, not a B7 item. Limitation: coordinator’s reading of published terms, not legal advice. The FCA drafting expresses the retrieval-system prohibition as subject to the personal/firm-use permission, which a lawyer might read more permissively; the synthetic route is adopted partly so that this ambiguity never has to be resolved. If licensed content is ever proposed for the corpus, that is the point at which a solicitor’s opinion is obtained, and no earlier.

* **DEC-0014 (2026-07-28) — Corpus manifest is a Git-versioned governed artefact with a single audit snapshot table (closed, July 2026).** Resolves the design question behind the first B7 required exit item (B7_GATE §3): where the corpus manifest lives and what `audit_run.corpus_version` — a column existing in a ratified, built, immutable schema — refers to, which until this decision was nothing. Reviewed by ChatGPT (approve with amendments, all adopted) and Grok (approve); the one material split (Q4, hash scope) was resolved by adopting ChatGPT's two-hash design, which satisfies Grok's underlying requirement — see below. **Adopted:** (1) `verticals/fraud/corpus/MANIFEST.yaml` is the sole governing artefact for corpus identity, licence, SA5 classification and eligibility. Vertical-side deliberately: corpus content is business semantics under ADR-0009; loader machinery (schema validation, hashing, snapshot insert) is platform-side. Document fields are split **identity** (id, source, licence per the DEC-0011 vocabulary `synthetic-arcaai` | `OGL-v3.0`, SA5 classification, content sha256, eligibility history) — immutable — versus **processing observations** (chunker_version, embedding_model, chunk_count, ingest_timestamp) — updatable with a manifest version change, never touching identity. Eligibility is a list of dated transitions (state ∈ included | pending_review | withdrawn | deprecated, date, reason); a mutable flag is prohibited. (2) One append-only runtime table `corpus_version` under the existing grant pattern: version_id (PK), human label `manifest_version` (ergonomic only, ignored for identity), `manifest_sha256`, `eligible_set_sha256`, `retrieval_snapshot_sha256`, `eligible_doc_count` (**derived metadata, cached for convenience — the manifest is authoritative and this value is never treated as truth**), `loaded_at`; CHECK constraints per the NULL-not-sentinel discipline. `audit_run.corpus_version` stores version_id. The snapshot row is evidence that a version was loaded; it is **not** a reconstruction of the manifest and must never be used to regenerate it. (3) **Two hashes, two questions** (panel Q4 resolution): `eligible_set_sha256` over the sorted eligible (doc_id, content_sha256) pairs answers "did these runs use the same documents"; `retrieval_snapshot_sha256` over the sorted (doc_id, content_sha256, chunker_version, embedding_model, chunk_count) tuples answers "did these runs retrieve from an operationally identical corpus". Grok's requirement that reproducibility be sensitive to chunking is met by the second hash; conflating both into one hash weakens both questions. (4) **Canonicalisation is normative:** deterministic UTF-8 serialisation with stable key ordering, LF endings, insignificant whitespace removed, before hashing — underspecified canonicalisation means two loaders can hash one manifest differently. (5) **Append-only is mechanical at load, not conventional:** against the previously loaded version of the manifest, the loader hard-fails on deleted transition entries, modified historical entries, or reordered transition history; appending a later transition is the only valid change to eligibility. (6) **No `git_commit` column** — considered and rejected (unanimous): a manifest's identity is its content hash; the commit is how a human finds the content, not what it is; recording it creates a second identity and couples replay to rewritable history. Replay chain: audit_run → corpus_version → manifest_sha256 → content-addressed lookup in the repo; `git log -S` locates the commit when a human wants context. (7) **Hash-pinning is the load-bearing tamper-evidence mechanism:** git history is append-only by policy, not mechanism (no branch protection; CL-E1 records a live force-push). At every load the loader recomputes `manifest_sha256` against the pinned row — mismatch is a hard failure. A scheduled ci-mlops step additionally re-hashes previously loaded versions (ChatGPT: load catches corruption before execution, CI before release; adopted now on cost grounds rather than deferred to Grok's external-contribution trigger, which instead becomes the point at which CL-E1's branch protection — prevention, not detection — is mandatory). Postgres is thereby tamper-evidence for git without becoming a second source of truth. **Rejected:** YAML-only (no queryable replay referent; transitions unenforced); four runtime tables `corpus_document`/`corpus_ingest_event`/`corpus_eligibility_transition`/`corpus_version` (governed configuration duplicated into SQL creates a second change-control system whose divergence from the repo is nobody's job to notice — the design needed a reconciliation export on day one, the tell that it was two systems; recorded because it is a natural elaboration of the correct audit-table pattern and will be proposed again); Postgres-as-master with generated YAML (inverts change control: the reviewed artefact would derive from unreviewed rows). **Consequences:** first B7 build items are the manifest schema + validator, seed manifest per DEC-0011, loader with snapshot insert and the mechanical append-only check, `corpus_version` model + grants + denial tests, and the ci-mlops re-hash step; the conftest GRANT_SQL duplication (2026-07-27 handover observation) is fixed in the same PR by executing `sql/governance_grants.sql` rather than a copy; CL-E1 rises from housekeeping to design dependency with a named trigger (first external contributor or client engagement → branch protection mandatory). **Standing constraint:** if the loader ever writes eligibility state into Postgres, or a convenience table reintroduces corpus state outside the manifest, the hybrid collapses into the rejected four-table anti-pattern — the constraint is stated in the loader contract and the schema docstring. Related: DEC-0011, DEC-0013, ADR-0009, ADR-0010, CL-E1, CF-1/B7-b, B7_GATE §3.

Work items track under WS-T deliverable naming within existing ledger discipline: decisions via the DEC series, architecture choices via the ADR series, work items via the WS-E ledger, all subject to the sequence-hold rule (WS-E 58). **First cargo is B7 exit evidence** — retrieval evaluation over the authored synthetic corpus, including detection of the two deliberate under-implementations (D1, D2) planted as gap-detection ground truth; serving-side behavioural regression (scoring, latency) is the second cargo, extending the B5 latency-gate lineage. **Two-regime test governance (TOR Section 5A) is the load-bearing constraint.** Every run belongs to exactly one regime, marked in its ledger entry and in its result artefact. Regime 1 (commissioning) proves the harness mechanism and says nothing about the system under test: its results are **inadmissible as gate evidence permanently, not merely until the Test Plan is ruled**, cannot be promoted retroactively, and a scenario whose result is wanted as evidence is re-run under Regime 2 — this bars the failure class WS-E 63 records, a green read as meaning something it cannot mean. ## Standards register (STD series)

**Authority.** Chair ruling of 2026-08-21, Option C, delivered at PROMPT 128R
and resolving the ID-scheme question escalated at PROMPT 128. M13 standards
take their own dedicated `STD-NNNN` series, hosted as ledger lines inside this
file and **not** as numbered files under `decisions/`. Option B — recording
standards as a class within the existing DEC/ADR numbering — was rejected
because standards are external, continuing obligations whose state changes on
external events, which an append-only decision log holds poorly; Option A, a
dedicated series as numbered files, was rejected on the scanner ground stated
next.

**Relationship to `scripts/repo_manifest.py`, stated BEFORE the first number is
consumed.** That scanner treats the `decisions/` filesystem as the ADR register
and reads the leading four digits of each filename there, and it parses the DEC
ledger from this file alone. The STD series is hosted here as ledger lines and
creates **no** numbered file under `decisions/`, so it consumes no ADR number —
including the 0011 reserved for Agentic Topology — and requires no change to
the scanner. This is the same mechanical reasoning DEC-0015 and DEC-0017 each
recorded when rejecting a numbered file for themselves. **The limit that
follows is stated rather than left to be discovered:** the scanner does not
parse the STD series either, so it will not report an STD numbering divergence
the way it reports DEC, ADR, CL and WS-E ones. Whether it is extended to read
this series is a separate act and is not performed here.

**Sequence-hold (WS-E 58) applies unchanged:** the next number is the highest
plus one, only. Ten numbers are consumed by this act; the next free number is
**next 0011**, cited as "next" and never bare, per the PR #85 convention.

**Numbering order, stated so it is reproducible.** Numbers are assigned in the
order of the closed framework vocabulary at `CLAUDE.md` queue item 34 M11 —
SS1/23, ISO42001, ISO27001, SOC2, NIST-AI-RMF, EU-AI-Act — and, within a
framework, by the instrument's own reference order. **Ordering by evidence
strength was considered and rejected:** evidence status changes as the build
advances while a number is permanent, so an evidence-ordered series would
encode a judgement that expires inside an identifier that does not.

**The claim this register makes, and the one it must never make.** Each entry
records which ArcaAI artefact **provides evidence supporting** an external
obligation. An artefact never **satisfies** one. The wording is inherited from
`docs/governance/SS1-23_PRINCIPLE_MAPPING.md` §3, ratified 2026-07-25:
obligations attach to firms, and evidence is what a platform can supply. Per
queue item 34 M11(a) the words *compliant*, *certified* and *conformant* may
not appear in an entry absent an actual certificate; that rule is not yet
mechanically enforced, which is itself the gap M11(a) names.

**Two state axes, deliberately not one.** `mapping_state` ∈ proposed /
ratified / deprecated / superseded / withdrawn records the standing of the
mapping; `evidence_status` ∈ EVIDENCED / PARTIAL / OWED / NONE / UNKNOWN
records whether the evidence exists. A single axis would let a ratified mapping
read as existing evidence — the failure shape at `CLAUDE.md` queue item 8. A
`PARTIAL` entry must enumerate both sides, and `UNKNOWN` never collapses into
green. **State changes are recorded as dated transitions with a reason, never
overwritten**, on the DEC-0014 corpus-eligibility and DEC-0012 model-inventory
discipline.
