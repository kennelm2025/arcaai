## 3. Derived requirements

**This section is derivation, not ruling.** Nothing below was decided by the
Chair in the transcribed text. It is the requirement set that the label
"QD-F2" is being used to denote, made explicit so that the design note can be
written against something stated rather than against an abbreviation. Where
the source is silent it is marked OPEN and not filled with judgement.

### 3.1 QD-F2 is two mechanisms, not one

The transcribed defect entry for F2 names a primary failure and then a second
under "Same family". They are distinct mechanisms with distinct triggers, and
the design note should treat them as two:

**(a) Refused-draft terminal state.** A refused draft currently stays RELEASED.
The next poll re-reads it, re-refuses it, and pages again — three pages in
three passes observed, and sixty per hour under the unattended loop. The
requirement is that a refused item reaches a terminal state: it leaves
RELEASED, it stops re-paging, and it carries its refusal report. The
driver's existing behaviour on refusal is already correct in every respect
except termination — `scripts/queue_driver.py` records that a refusal produces
"an ERROR page, a log row, and no route", and that "none is retried, because
none is transient". The defect is not that the driver retries; it is that the
*input* is never marked, so each poll presents the same doubt afresh.

**(b) Processed archive or high-water mark for swept outcome files.** Swept
outcome files have no terminal state and re-alert. The requirement is the same
shape as (a) applied to the return leg: a swept outcome reaches a terminal
state and does not re-alert. The transcribed text names the remedy as
"processed archive / high-water mark" without choosing between them.

The corroboration is worth recording because it is unusually strong for a
defect of this kind: the re-alerting behaviour was **found independently three
times** — by lane T2, by the driver log, and by the Chair's mobile session.

### 3.2 The withholding condition, restated

The 60-second unattended loop is WITHHELD until QD-F2 lands. Single-pass and
attended operation are authorised now. **Lifting the withholding is a Chair
ruling, not a consequence of the code landing** — a candidate for the next
free DEC number, cited here as next 0020 and not as a claim that the entry
exists.

The reasoning DEC-0019 records for keeping this in the register rather than
only in a session record applies directly to this document: a repeating loop
that re-pages on a refused draft is an escalation channel training its reader
to ignore it. QD-F2 is therefore not a tidiness fix. It is the precondition
for the escalation channel meaning anything at all once nobody is at the
prompt.

### 3.4 OPEN — where the source is silent

Marked rather than answered, per the transcription discipline.

1. **OPEN-1 — where the terminal state lives.** The source requires that a
   refused draft stop re-paging; it does not say whether the marker is a Gmail
   label, a filesystem move, or both. The driver deletes nothing, ever, so any
   design must reach terminal state without deletion.
2. **OPEN-2 — archive versus high-water mark.** The source names both and
   chooses neither. They are not equivalent: an archive is per-item and
   survives reordering; a high-water mark is positional and assumes monotonic
   sweep.
3. **OPEN-3 — what the high-water mark is keyed on**, if that route is taken —
   prompt number, sweep timestamp, or message id. Prompt numbers are known
   non-monotonic across the series fork recorded at
   `docs/governance/PROMPT_SERIES_RECONCILIATION_2026-08-22.md`, which bears
   directly on this choice.
4. **OPEN-4 — where the refusal report travels.** The requirement that a
   refused item "carries its refusal report" does not say to whom, or whether
   the report is the existing ERROR page, the log row, or a third artefact.
5. **OPEN-5 — retention and expiry.** Whether terminal-state items are ever
   cleared, by what act, and by whom. Unstated.
6. **OPEN-6 — the commissioning checklist is not in the tree.** The ruling adds
   a TLS dual-stack rule and an OAuth test-user gate to it. The checklist
   itself is not carried in the envelope and no in-tree artefact was found for
   it, so the two additions are transcribed without their target. Same M2 shape
   as the source draft itself.
7. **OPEN-7 — whether (a) and (b) land as one act or two.** The source treats
   them as one family under one label; it does not rule that they land
   together. Deferred to PROMPT 156.

## 4. Cross-references

### 4.1 The governing register entry

DEC-0019 adopts the driver and carries the operational-status note that
withholds the loop. Its clauses bearing on any QD-F2 design: the driver never
runs git and never imports `subprocess`; every write is inside the queue root;
it deletes nothing, ever; refusal is the default on doubt; and the escalation
topic is treated as a credential. A terminal-state mechanism must be
constructible without violating any of them — in particular the
deletes-nothing property, which rules out the simplest implementation of an
archive.

### 4.2 Live instances of the refusal shape — now four, and citable

The refusal path is no longer theoretical. Four instances exist:

1. and 2. **The two 21 August mis-window refusals of PROMPT 139.** Manual
   carriage left them as terminal-only text — which is the defect in vivo: the
   refusal was correct, and it terminated nowhere the driver could see.
3. and 4. **The two 22 August BLOCKED-RULINGs** — the refusal of the envelope
   staged as PROMPT 141, and the STEP-0 rooting halt. Their manual disposition
   prototyped a terminal-state filesystem shape that is now live in the queue
   root: `_queue\T1\dead\` holding the refused item, and `_queue\T1\done\`
   holding the discharged one.

**Cite that layout as the design's empirical starting point, not as ruled
layout.** It was produced by hand under two specific dispositions and no
ruling establishes it. Its value is that it is the only observed instance of
the shape the design must automate, which is a better starting point than an
invented one and a worse one than a ruled one.

### 4.3 F1 has a live in-transport instance — this envelope's own staging

The 21 August staged draft of this very envelope carried a bare outcome-file
path, and Gmail corrupted it into an invented `http://` redirect URL. That is
defect F1 occurring in transport, on the document that specifies its own
register. It is cited in the F1 sibling entry at section 3.3 for that reason:
the defect reproduces on ordinary governance traffic, not on a contrived
input.

### 4.4 The F5 brief

`docs/governance/F5_DESIGN_BRIEF_2026-08-21.md` OPEN-4 is closed by this
document. The brief's warning about the label collision stands and is
reinforced here: the queue driver's F2 and the governance guard's F2 are
unrelated and collide only in label. The guard's F2 taught write-detection to
distinguish a descriptor duplication from a file redirection. The driver's F2
is the subject of this document. Any text citing a bare "F2" is ambiguous
between them.
