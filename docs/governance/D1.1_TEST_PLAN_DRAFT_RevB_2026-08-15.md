## 2. Coverage matrix

### 2.1 The coverage rule, stated as class-specific obligations

**The Cartesian-product wording of Rev A is WITHDRAWN.** Rev A stated coverage as
every scenario class × every document series, and its appendix never instantiated
that rule: it generated seven corpus-QA scenarios against six series and two each
of the other classes, then claimed series coverage satisfied the product. The
appendix was the honest artefact and the rule was the overstatement. What follows
is the defensible rule made explicit (`F-DS-04` point 1, discharging `F-GROK-01`,
`F-CHATGPT-01` and its dissent, `F-GEMINI-01` and `F-GROK-05`).

Coverage is three obligations, one per class, each with named exclusions carrying
reasons.

**Obligation A — corpus QA.** Every document series represented in the current
listed snapshot's eligible set carries **at least one** corpus-QA scenario.

**Obligation B — citation-following.** Every document series that emits at least
one intra-corpus citation carries **at least one** citation-following scenario
whose source document belongs to that series. Series emitting no intra-corpus
citation are named exclusions with reasons.

**Obligation C — gap detection.** Every planted under-implementation in the
ground-truth set carries exactly one scenario. The set is D1 and D2 (`TOR §1:17`),
so this obligation is two scenarios and is complete by construction.

The obligations are expressed as rules rather than fixed lists because
`TOR §3:30` requires a coverage matrix and not a scenario count. A rule stays
true as the corpus grows; a fixed list is stale at the next inclusion decision
and would require a plan amendment, which after panel acceptance is itself a
governed act (`TOR §5A:101`). The instantiated batch-1 set generated from the
obligations is at Appendix A, and is an appendix precisely so that regenerating
it is not an amendment to the matrix.

**Obligation B stands in its stronger form, and the weaker one is recorded as
withdrawn.** A direction-based obligation — one scenario per structural kind of
edge, within-series and cross-series, two scenarios in all — was drafted first
and is **WITHDRAWN by chair ruling of 2026-08-15**. Rationale, on the record:
acceptance safety outweighs authoring cost, and a knowingly weaker obligation
invites a MATERIAL finding from a panel that has already once forced this very
rule to be restated.

The per-series form **loses nothing the direction-based form covered.** Both
structural kinds of edge remain represented across the six scenarios, as
Appendix A.2 shows, because every emitting series carries both within-series and
cross-series edges. What it adds is that each series must demonstrate its own
outbound edges rather than resting on a scenario built over another series'.

**Scenario batch 1 is retrieval over the fraud corpus** — corpus QA,
citation-following, and D1/D2 gap detection — because it is the B7 exit
evidence; scoring scenarios follow as batch 2 (Ruling 2, OQ2, confirmed from
Rev B of the TOR and unanimous; `TOR §10:147`).

### 2.4 The typology gap, named rather than papered over

**`TOR §3:30` requires a coverage matrix per fraud typology. This plan does not
deliver one, and the matrix at Appendix A is PROVISIONAL against that
requirement.** Rev A substituted document series for typology and proceeded as
though the requirement were satisfied; a series is not a typology, and the
substitution is withdrawn (`F-DS-04` points 1 and 2).

**Stated in terms, because it is the point of the label: a Regime 2 run against
the current manifest is NOT evidence of typology coverage.** It is evidence of
coverage over document series, which is a different and weaker claim. No gate
record, prospect pack or evidence citation may present the one as the other while
this label stands.

**The mechanical cause.** The manifest carries no structured series or typology
field. Series membership is embedded in prose inside the `source:` string, and
fraud typology is not carried at all. The matrix cannot therefore be generated or
checked mechanically against the manifest, because the fields it would key on do
not exist as fields. Series assignment in this plan is read from `source:` prose
and maintained by hand, which introduces manual error into an otherwise automated
rule (`F-GEMINI-01`).

**Two mechanisms narrow the gap without closing it.**

**Per-scenario typology declaration is REQUIRED** (`F-DS-04` point 3). Every
scenario spec declares the fraud typology it covers, so typology coverage is
assessable at spec level now and reconciles with the corpus when the manifest
field lands. **This needs no schema change:**
`arcaai/harness/schema/scenario_spec_v0.2.schema.json` already carries `typology`
as a required top-level field — string, minimum length 1, in the required array
alongside `scenario_class` and `vertical`. Every valid spec already declares it.

**A controlled vocabulary is required, or coverage cannot be computed**, and this
plan states where it lives. The schema describes `typology` as an opaque string
for the same reason as `vertical` — the ADR-0009 boundary, which bars platform
machinery from encoding vertical semantics. Opacity means two specs may write
`card-not-present` and `CNP fraud` and a coverage check sees two typologies where
there is one. **The vocabulary therefore lives vertical-side, in a fraud-vertical
artefact alongside the corpus, and never in the schema.** Adding an enum to
`arcaai/harness/schema/scenario_spec_v0.2.schema.json` is the instinctive fix and
is the one place it must not go, because it would put vertical semantics inside
platform machinery in breach of `decisions/0009-platform-vertical-boundary.md`.
The artefact itself is owed and is recorded at Appendix B with the corpus
workstream as owner.

**The manifest typology field remains the real dependency** and is DEFERRED WITH
AN OWNER: it is a `DEC-0014` question for the corpus workstream, ruled at its own
stop (`F-DS-04` point 5). Per-scenario declaration makes typology coverage
assessable at spec level; it does not make it assessable against the corpus,
because nothing connects a declared typology to the documents that ground it
until the manifest carries the field. **The PROVISIONAL label is not lifted by
per-scenario declaration or by the coverage audit event.** It is lifted when the
manifest carries typology and the matrix can be computed against it.

### 2.5 Coverage audit event

**Any change to the eligible set that introduces a new series raises a ledgered
coverage audit event** (`F-DS-04` point 4). Silent appendix refresh is barred: an
appendix regenerated without a ledger entry is indistinguishable from one that
was never out of date, and the dynamic form of the coverage rule is only
defensible while every regeneration is a governed act.

The audit event records the movement in the eligible set, the series introduced
or removed, and the resulting appendix revision. It is the control that answers
the alternative-perspective objection ruled at `G-ALT-REG`: a regulator's
preference for version-locked test-case lists per gate is met by the hash-pinned
instantiated appendix at each accepted revision, with the rule governing growth
between revisions and every regeneration ledgered.

### 2.6 Vertical coverage

One vertical is in scope: fraud. The plan is written so that a second vertical
adds rows to the matrix rather than requiring a new plan, consistent with the
ADR-0009 boundary that keeps the schema and harness free of vertical semantics.
The typology vocabulary at §2.4 is vertical-side for the same reason, and a
second vertical brings its own.

## 3. Retrieval scenario classes

### 3.1 The three classes

Ruled at OQ2 and closed for batch 1. Each asks a different question of the same
corpus.

| Class | The question it asks | Schema `retrieval_kind` |
|---|---|---|
| **Corpus QA** | When the corpus contains the grounding a question requires, is that material what comes back? | `corpus_qa` |
| **Citation-following** | When a document refers to another, does the retriever reach the referent? | `citation_following` |
| **Gap detection** | When the corpus does *not* contain the grounding, does the system say so rather than answer anyway? | `gap_detection` |

### 3.2 Class does not determine scoring method, and the asymmetry is deliberate

The schema carries three retrieval kinds and **two** scoring methods
(`arcaai/harness/schema/scenario_spec_v0.2.schema.json`). Corpus QA and
citation-following both score by `semantic_distance` — recall at k over an
expected grounding set. Only gap detection scores by `gap_detection`.

This is not an omission. Corpus QA and citation-following are both presence
questions and differ in how the expected set is *chosen*, not in how success is
measured: for corpus QA the expected set is the material that grounds the
question, for citation-following it is the referent of a citation. Gap detection
measures absence and needs its own formula, for the reason §4 sets out.

### 3.3 Statute retrieval — candidate class, not created at batch 1

A distinct statute-retrieval class was considered following F1, and is **not
created for batch 1**. The class set is ruled at OQ2 and closed; more
substantially, creating a scenario class to explain a single observation risks
encoding that observation as a design fact before it is understood, and the
observation comes from a permanently inadmissible run (§12).

It is recorded as a candidate. The coverage obligations at §2.1 will expose
whether statute documents behave differently from synthetic narrative documents
under one similarity measure, because Obligation A puts both in the matrix.

## 4. Gap detection and retrieval quality are distinct claims

### 4.1 The governing rule

This plan preserves the distinction between a scenario detecting a deliberately
planted under-implementation and any general claim about retrieval quality.
D1/D2 detection evidences gap-detection capability; it does not by itself
evidence retrieval quality broadly (`amendment 2`, at
`docs/governance/RULINGS_RECORD_2026-08-10_TOR-test-capability.md:60`). The
TOR's own acceptance criterion restates it: gap-detection scenarios demonstrate
detection, or recorded non-detection with analysis, of D1 and D2 "as Regime 2
runs, evidencing gap-detection capability distinctly from general retrieval
quality" (`TOR §8:123`).

### 4.2 Why these are two claims and not one

D1 and D2 are two under-implementations planted deliberately as gap-detection
ground truth (`TOR §1:17`). A gap-detection scenario asks whether the system is
honest under absence. A corpus-QA or citation-following scenario asks whether
the retriever ranks well under presence.

A system can hold one and not the other in either direction: a retriever that
returns almost nothing scores well on abstention and badly on recall; a
retriever that always returns its top k confidently scores well on recall and
fails every planted gap. Neither result licenses an inference about the other,
because neither measurement is taken over the other's conditions.

### 4.3 The mechanical basis, already in the schema

Semantic-distance scoring is recall at k over the expected grounding set, and
the schema states that this "scores PRESENCE: how much of what should have been
retrieved was retrieved". Gap-detection scoring is a weighted combination of
abstention and distractor avoidance, neither term a semantic distance. The
schema's own sentence is the anchor: "a gap-detection scenario that scored well
on semantic distance would be measuring the opposite of what it is for".

The exclusivity is mechanical. `expected_document_ids` is "meaningless for
gap_detection, where by construction no document grounds the claim", and the
gap-detection branch forbids it outright, just as the semantic-distance branch
forbids the gap-detection scoring block. A spec cannot express a scenario that
claims both at once.

### 4.4 Consequence for evidence

A gap-detection result, run under Regime 2 and meeting its entry criteria
(`TOR §5A:99`), is citable as evidence of gap-detection capability against the
named ground truth. It is **not** citable as evidence that retrieval works. A
corpus-QA or citation-following result is citable as evidence of retrieval
quality on the query and grounding set it was run over, at its pinned snapshot —
and is **not** citable as evidence that the system abstains correctly where
grounding is absent. A gate claim spanning both requires results of both classes.

Both claims are additionally bounded by §2.4: neither is evidence of typology
coverage while the matrix carries its PROVISIONAL label.

### 4.5 The failure mode this prevents

A suite that detects both planted gaps being read, at a gate or in a prospect
pack, as a general statement that retrieval works. It is attractive precisely
because it reads well: two deliberate defects found, ground truth matched,
nothing missed. But the evidence produced is entirely about behaviour under
absence — every document the scenario touches is a distractor, and by
construction no document would have been correct to retrieve.

## 5. Pass/fail criteria

**This section is rebuilt rather than amended.** Six adopted obligations from
cluster C2 land here, and a section patched six times by hand is how a rule and
its worked examples drift apart (`R-3`).

### 5.1 Where a threshold lives

Per scenario, in the spec. The schema carries `acceptance` as
`{metric, operator, value}`, with the operator enumerated rather than free text
"so the gate is evaluated, not interpreted". This plan states how a threshold is
**chosen** and does not restate any number the schema carries — the same
division `amendment 3` makes for migration-diff semantics, and for the same
reason: a rule stated in two places drifts.

### 5.2 How the expected set is constituted, which governs before any threshold

A threshold rule that governs how the bar follows from the expected set E, while
saying nothing about how E is constituted, controls the wrong half of the
problem: a scenario can materially change its acceptance difficulty by defining
grounding at a different granularity while complying perfectly with the threshold
rule (`F-CHATGPT-02`).

**E's composition and granularity are reviewed at scenario acceptance,
independently of the threshold arithmetic**, against four stated criteria. A
scenario whose E fails any of them is not accepted, whatever its threshold.

1. **Granularity is document-level.** E is a set of document identifiers, not
   chunks or passages. The metric is computed over documents, so an E expressed
   at another granularity states a criterion the metric cannot evaluate.
2. **Sufficiency.** Every document in E is one whose absence from the retrieved
   set would make the answer ungrounded. A document that merely mentions the
   subject is not grounding, and padding E with such documents lowers the real
   bar while raising the apparent one.
3. **Necessity.** No document is omitted from E that the query's answer depends
   on. Trimming E to the most-retrievable subset raises the apparent score
   without changing retrieval, and is the mirror of criterion 2.
4. **Independence from observed output.** E is fixed before any retrieval is run
   for that scenario, and is recorded in the spec at authoring. An E adjusted
   after seeing what the retriever returned is not a criterion but a description.

**Marginal cases are noted with reasons at scenario acceptance:** where a
document's inclusion in or exclusion from E is a judgment rather than a clear
application of criteria 2 and 3, that judgment and its reason are recorded with
the scenario.

Criterion 4 is the same discipline §8.4 applies to session scope and §12.2 applies
to F1: a bar chosen with the result already visible has stopped being a control.

### 5.3 Class-level default thresholds

A default gives the panel something to interrogate and the scenario author a
baseline rather than a blank page.

**Semantic-distance scenarios (corpus QA, citation-following).**

The metric is recall at k over the expected grounding set E, so its granularity
is `1/|E|` and its attainable values are exactly the multiples of `1/|E|`. A
threshold that is not such a multiple is misleading: it silently rounds to the
next attainable value while reading as though it were finer.

> **Default threshold = `ceil(0.8 × |E|) / |E|`.**

Worked: at `|E| = 1` the default is 1.0; at `|E| = 2, 3, 4` it is also 1.0; at
`|E| = 5` it is 0.8; at `|E| = 8` it is 0.875.

**`|E| ≤ 4` is a binary probe, not a graded measure, and Rev A's graded-measure
language is WITHDRAWN for those cases** (`F-GROK-02`). Rev A declared only
`|E| = 1` a binary probe while applying the same rule to 2, 3 and 4 and retaining
graded language; four of its seven corpus-QA scenarios were affected. The
declaration extends to **every scenario whose attainable values under the default
are only {0, 1}**, which is exactly `|E| ≤ 4`. Such a scenario is described as a
binary probe throughout its spec and its result, and **any `|E| ≤ 4` requires a
stated justification that the binary form is intentional rather than accidental**.
`top_k` is the sensitivity lever there, not the threshold.

**Gap-detection scenarios.**

The score is `w_a·A + w_d·(1 − |R_k ∩ D|/|D|)` with `w_a + w_d = 1`, A binary.

> **Default threshold = 0.8.**

At the fixture weighting `w_a = 0.6, w_d = 0.4`, that default requires the
system to abstain *and* to admit no more than half the distractor set. Two
properties worth stating because they are not obvious from the number: any
threshold above `w_d` makes abstention **necessary** — a system that answers
confidently cannot pass however few distractors it returns; and any threshold
above `w_a` additionally constrains distractor avoidance.

**Weights and threshold are a COUPLED PAIR** (`F-DS-08`). The 0.8 default is
calibrated to `w_a = 0.6, w_d = 0.4`; under other weights the
abstention-necessary property may not hold. **Changing either requires restating
both in the spec, with the abstention-necessary property explicitly checked and
the check recorded.** A weight change that silently voids the property would
leave a scenario passing while a confidently wrong answer went undetected.

### 5.4 What recall at k does not claim, and the precision boundary

**Recall at k is a PRESENCE metric.** It claims neither precision nor rank
quality: it treats every expected document as equally important, carries no
rank-order term, and has no distractor-avoidance term at all. Where `|E| > k` it
cannot distinguish retrieving the correct k from retrieving any k (`F-DS-01`).

> **At `|E| ≥ 5`, or in any scenario where distractor contamination bears on the
> claim being made, an additional precision-or-rank criterion is REQUIRED
> alongside the recall threshold.**

**The boundary is `|E| = 5` and it is shared with §5.3 deliberately.** The default
`ceil(0.8 × |E|) / |E|` returns 1.0 at `|E|` of 1, 2, 3 and 4 and first returns a
non-unit value at `|E| = 5`. So the binary-probe rule covers `|E| ≤ 4` and the
precision obligation begins at `|E| ≥ 5`: the two partition the range at one
point, with no gap and no overlap. **The two rules are stated together here for
that reason** — moving one boundary without the other produces either a band of
scenarios that are binary and carry no precision obligation, or one that is graded
and carries two (`R-3`).

**Bound on `top_k`, expressed as a ratio.** With thresholds at 1.0 for `|E| ≤ 4`,
sensitivity rests entirely on `top_k`, which Rev A left unbounded relative to
corpus size; an excessive `top_k` produces a trivial pass with a noise-heavy
payload (`F-GEMINI-02`).

> **`top_k` may not exceed 10% of the indexed chunk count of the eligible set
> without a mandatory flag on the spec and a stated reason.**

Against the current denominator of 71 chunks: `top_k` 5 is 7.0% and passes
unflagged; `top_k` 10 is 14.1% and requires the flag; `top_k` 20 is 28.2% and
would return more than a quarter of the entire corpus.

**The bound is a ratio rather than an absolute because the denominator will
move.** Thirty documents are listed and fourteen sit at `pending_review`; a
`top_k` that is 28% of the corpus today could be 15% after the inclusion decision
without anyone having changed it. An absolute bound would silently loosen as the
corpus grew, which is the direction that matters — a bound that relaxes itself is
not a bound.

### 5.5 Departures from the default are ruled variances

**A departure from a default threshold is a RULED VARIANCE requiring operator
approval, and is recorded as such** (`F-DS-02`, option (b)). A stated reason in
the spec is **not** sufficient. Rev A permitted a per-scenario override carrying
a stated reason, with no sufficiency criterion for that reason and therefore no
account of what an override is for; the quantisation argument at §5.3 makes the
default the only threshold expressing 0.8 exactly, so a departure is a
substantive change to the bar rather than a refinement of it.

The same variance discipline applies to evidence substitution at §7.2, and the two
are the same instrument applied to the two places where this plan permits a
departure from its own rule.

**Already covered, recorded explicitly so the coverage is visible rather than
accidental:** a threshold change also invalidates that scenario's prior results,
by a route already in the plan. The threshold lives in the spec file, the runner
computes `spec_sha` over the raw spec bytes, and §9.2's spec-hash row invalidates
the scenario's prior results on any spec-hash change. So a threshold edit,
variance included, already invalidates what preceded it. Stated here because a
control that works by accident is one refactor away from not working.

### 5.6 What evaluates acceptance, and where the verdict is recorded (H-8)

**H-8 was deferred to its proper stop and this is that stop**, because Regime 2
becomes live when this plan is ruled (`TOR §5A:92-93`). Ruled 2026-08-14:

> **The runner evaluates.** The verdict is written to the result artefact
> (D3.1) **and** to the D2.5 results ledger entry, and is **immutable in the
> ledger by the same mechanism that makes the regime marker immutable**.

**Grounds, recorded because the ruling binds runner design.** H-10 ruled the
run-record obligations machine-asserted under Regime 2 where they had been human
records under Regime 1; acceptance evaluation is the same shape. H-9 ruled the
regime marker mechanically immutable because "a marker a process can edit is a
promotion path" — and the pass/fail verdict is the field most likely to be
quoted away from its artefact, so the argument applies with more force, not
less. `TOR §3:32` already describes the runner as "scored/evaluated".

**Two conditions, both ruled in.**

1. **`evaluated` is always present and explicit.** An absent verdict must never
   read as a pass. This mirrors the discipline the runner already applies to an
   unpinned retrieval snapshot, where absence is reported as unpinned rather
   than passed over.
2. **An inadmissible run's verdict is marked inadmissible, never suppressed.**
   Deleting it loses the fact that a verdict was computed; marking it preserves
   the record while `TOR §5A:99` bars its use.

### 5.7 The evaluator is verified before use, and the verdict is bound to its bytes

The runner both scores and issues the verdict. Immutability closes post-hoc edit
attacks; it does nothing about a defective comparison inside the runner, which
would be recorded immutably and wrongly (`F-GROK-03`). Three requirements close
that triangle, and none of them closes it alone: **verified before use**,
**tamper-evident in output**, **governed in failure**.

**(a) Golden-fixture suite, and it is an entry criterion.** An independent,
versioned golden-fixture set asserts the threshold comparison: known inputs,
known verdicts, both pass and fail shapes, and boundary cases at the quantisation
points of §5.3. **The fixture suite must be GREEN before any Regime 2 run is
admissible.** It joins §8.1 as the sixth entry criterion and is machine-asserted
under H-10; its assertion surface is named at §8.1 rather than left implicit,
because it would otherwise arrive in the same unnamed condition as the four
criteria `F-GROK-07` found.

**(b) The result artefact is content-addressed.** The D3.1 result artefact
carries the threshold, `|E|` and `top_k`, and Rev A attached immutability to the
ledger entry only. The artefact is content-addressed and **the immutable D2.5
ledger entry carries the artefact's hash**, giving the invariant in terms:

> **The immutable verdict can always be shown to refer to exactly the result
> bytes that produced it.**

This invents no mechanism. Content-addressing is already this repository's ruled
identity discipline — `DEC-0014` rejects a `git_commit` column outright because
"a manifest's identity is its content hash", and the runner already computes
`spec_sha` over raw spec bytes. Duplicate storage of the verdict in two places is
not required and is not wanted; the hash is the relationship.

**(c) A confirmed evaluator defect has a governed consequence**, at §9.2 row 8.

### 5.8 Recording rule

**A threshold, the expected-set size `|E|`, and `top_k` travel together in the
result artefact.** A threshold quoted without the other two is not
interpretable: 0.8 means different things at `|E| = 2` and `|E| = 8`, and a
recall figure without `top_k` does not say what was searched. Where the `top_k`
flag of §5.4 is set, it travels with them.

## 6. Data provenance rules

This section states where every byte a scenario touches comes from, how that
origin is identified, and what a result must carry for the origin to be
recoverable. Which movement in those facts invalidates which prior results is
§9.

### 6.1 Two scenario classes, two provenance lines

**Retrieval-class scenarios query the authored corpus at a pinned snapshot.**
`generator_seed` is FORBIDDEN on a retrieval-class spec, because "a seed
recorded against a run that never used one is a reproducibility claim with
nothing behind it".

**Scoring-class scenarios materialise records from the in-house synthetic
generator at a declared seed.** `generator_seed` is mandatory there, in the spec
and in the reproducibility payload (`amendment 6`). A scoring-class spec may
additionally pin a corpus snapshot; if it does, only the manifest pair is
required of it, never the retrieval-snapshot hash, "which would describe an
index the scenario never queries".

Real, non-synthetic data of any kind is out of scope for the whole capability
(`TOR §3:43`). Scenario content may be LLM-assisted at the design layer only;
all volume data is produced programmatically from versioned specs, with external
model outputs treated as distinct provenance lines, schema-validated before
pipeline entry and hash-pinned (`TOR §2:24`).

### 6.2 Corpus identity has exactly one governing artefact

`verticals/fraud/corpus/MANIFEST.yaml` is the sole governing artefact for corpus
identity, licence, SA5 classification and eligibility (`DEC-0014` item 1).

**The identity boundary, stated once and in one vocabulary.** `DEC-0014` and the
manifest's own header describe the split in different words — the decision places
eligibility history inside an immutable identity block, while the manifest lists
five immutable identity fields and gives eligibility its own append-only rule.
These are reconcilable and neither is wrong, but *immutable* and *append-only*
are distinct properties and this plan uses them distinctly:

- **Immutable** — the value never changes once listed. This covers `id`,
  `source`, `licence`, `sa5_classification` and `content_sha256`. A revised
  source document is a new entry with a new hash, never an edit.
- **Append-only** — existing entries never change, and the only permitted change
  is the addition of a later entry. This covers the eligibility transition list.
  Deleting, modifying or reordering a historical transition is a hard load
  failure; a mutable eligibility flag is prohibited (`DEC-0014` items 1 and 5).
- **Updatable with a manifest version change** — the processing observations
  `chunker_version`, `embedding_model`, `chunk_count` and `ingest_timestamp`,
  which never touch identity.

The distinction is load-bearing for §9.2 row 3: an appended eligibility
transition changes the manifest and therefore `manifest_sha256`, while changing
no document's `content_sha256`. Collapsing append-only into immutable is exactly
the reading that makes that row look unreachable, and §9.2 records why it is not.

The `corpus_version` Postgres row is evidence that a version was loaded. It is
not a reconstruction of the manifest and must never be used to regenerate one
(`DEC-0014` item 2).

### 6.3 Three hashes, three different questions

- **Manifest hash** — over the normative canonical form: deterministic UTF-8,
  stable key ordering, LF endings, insignificant whitespace removed
  (`DEC-0014` item 4). Answers *is this the same manifest content?*
- **Eligible-set hash** — over the sorted eligible document-and-content-hash
  pairs. Answers *did these runs use the same documents?* It deliberately
  excludes processing facts (`DEC-0014` item 3), and that exclusion is the point
  of having two hashes: it lets a re-chunk be recognised as *the same
  documents*, so document drift and processing drift stay separable.
- **Retrieval-snapshot hash** — the eligible pairs plus chunker version,
  embedding model and chunk count (`DEC-0014` item 3). Answers *did these runs
  retrieve from an operationally identical corpus?* Required for retrieval-class
  scenarios at spec schema v0.2.

`DEC-0014` item 3 states why they are not one hash: "conflating both into one
hash weakens both questions."

**Both content hashes range over eligible documents only.** Anything done to a
non-eligible document moves the manifest hash and neither content hash. This is
the mechanical basis of §9.2 row 3 and is stated here rather than only there.

### 6.4 "Recorded processing facts" means three fields, not four

**Defined once, because two adopted findings collide on it** (`F-GROK-04` read
with the `F-DS-05` verification):

> **Throughout this plan, "recorded processing facts" means `chunker_version`,
> `embedding_model` and `chunk_count` — the three the retrieval-snapshot hash
> covers. It does NOT include `ingest_timestamp`.**

`DEC-0014` names four processing observations; the retrieval-snapshot hash folds
in three. The exclusion of `ingest_timestamp` is correct and deliberate: a
timestamp would make every re-ingest look like processing drift, and a re-ingest
producing identical chunker version, embedding model and chunk count has changed
nothing the retriever sees.

**The consequence if this definition were omitted is concrete.** §9.2 row 2 fires
on any change to recorded processing facts. Drafted against the manifest's four
fields, it would fire on a re-ingest timestamp and invalidate every
retrieval-class result for no reason at all.

### 6.5 Known limit of the retrieval-snapshot pin

The platform's hash function stringifies absent processing facts, so the hash
computes cleanly over documents carrying no processing block. A spec can
therefore satisfy the v0.2 requirement with a correct, matching hash that folds
in absent values — a required pin with nothing behind it, which is the defect
the requirement exists to close. **Making the pin mandatory does not make the
facts present.**

**Currently latent, verified 2026-08-14 and re-verified 2026-08-15:** every one
of the sixteen eligible documents in `manifest_version` 2026-08-13.8 carries a
complete processing block, so no eligible document is presently affected. The
limit is real and unclosed; it is not presently biting. That distinction is
recorded so a later reader neither dismisses the limit nor assumes it is
currently corrupting results.

### 6.6 Listed versus current snapshots

Commissioning (Regime 1) runs pin the snapshot current at run time
(`amendment 9`; `TOR §5A:88`). Formal (Regime 2) runs require the corpus at a
**listed** snapshot (`TOR §5A:94`). The asymmetry is deliberate: commissioning
proves plumbing and is permanently inadmissible, so an unlisted snapshot costs
nothing; formal execution is evidence, and evidence must cite a snapshot that
passed the governed listing act.

**Listed is not the same as eligible**, and §2.3 records a live instance:
fourteen documents are listed at `pending_review` and are invisible to the
retriever. A Regime 2 run satisfies the listed-snapshot criterion while covering
only the eligible subset, and the coverage obligations at §2.1 operate over
eligibility for that reason.

### 6.7 Immutability of the source data

Authoring and listing are separate governed acts, and nothing under
`verticals/fraud/corpus/` changes outside one. Authored corpus documents are
immutable once committed: a correction is a new entry with a new hash, never an
edit, and a revised or withdrawn document is an appended eligibility transition,
never a deletion — enforced mechanically at load (`DEC-0014` item 5). Data
itself never enters git. Provenance for scoring-class inputs is therefore a
recipe — generator plus seed plus parameter ranges — not a stored file, which is
why the seed is mandatory rather than advisory.

### 6.8 What a result must carry: the reproducibility identity

**The reproducibility identity has four legs**, and every result carries all
four, plus a generator seed wherever the generator is invoked:

1. **Spec hash** — `spec_sha`, over the raw spec bytes.
2. **Model version** — see the artefact-resolution requirement below.
3. **Corpus snapshot** — the `DEC-0014` manifest hash pair, with the
   retrieval-snapshot hash additionally required for retrieval-class scenarios at
   schema v0.2.
4. **Evaluator version** — the version of the evaluation logic that computed the
   verdict.

**The fourth leg is new at Rev B and is a chair ruling of 2026-08-15**, carrying
in the structural option DeepSeek proposed at `F-DS-03`. The recorded rationale:
the identity already carries heavy weight, and leaving the evaluator as the one
moving part outside it is the weaker position — **a change to evaluation logic
must be visible in the pin set, never silent.**

**Its reach is stated rather than discovered.** `TOR §5:74` and `TOR §8:124` both
state the reproducibility requirement as three legs plus a seed. The fourth leg
**adds to** that requirement and does not amend the TOR: the TOR states a floor,
and amendments land as requirements on this plan rather than on the TOR text
(rulings record §4:57). The leg also binds the D3.1 result artefact and the
runner build, which is where it is implemented; it is recorded at Appendix B with
that owner.

**Evaluation logic is separately versioned.** It is versioned independently of
the runner as a whole, so that a runner release which does not touch the
evaluator does not present as an evaluator change, and an evaluator change inside
an unchanged runner release cannot hide. §9.2 rows 7 and 8 give the version its
consequences.

**Model version must resolve to an immutable artefact** (`F-CHATGPT-05`). "Model
version stated" is weaker than the pinned-identity discipline used everywhere
else in this plan, and Rev A did not establish whether the identifier resolved to
an immutable artefact or was merely a label. **The identifier carried by a result
must resolve to an immutable, content-addressed model artefact.** The DVC
artefact store is the anchor: `decisions/0007-artefact-store-dvc.md` rules DVC
the artefact source-of-truth, and `data/fraud/models/xgb_mvm.ubj` with
`platt_scaler.json` are DVC-pinned rather than MLflow-registered for exactly this
reason.

A result missing any leg of its identity is not a weaker result; it is an
unreproducible one, and is inadmissible under `TOR §5A:99`.

## 7. Gate linkage: harness evidence and B7 exit

### 7.1 The governing ruling

Ruling 3 (OQ3) is preferred-primary, the middle position: "Harness Regime-2 runs
against the ruled Test Plan are the preferred primary evidence for retrieval
quality and for detection of the planted D1/D2 gaps. They are not a hard
single-point dependency that can block the entire B7 gate if equivalent citable
evidence exists by another governed route"
(`docs/governance/RULINGS_RECORD_2026-08-10_TOR-test-capability.md:42`). The
operator's recorded rationale is that making the gate wholly dependent on a
brand-new harness creates a single point of failure during the harness's own
early life, while making it merely nice-to-have under-weights the capability
being built (same file, line 43).

### 7.2 What preferred-primary means operationally

**(a) First call.** Where a B7 exit item can be evidenced by a retrieval-class
scenario, the harness is the route of first resort, and the Gate Acceptance
Record cites the Regime-2 run artefact by its reproducibility identity (§6.8).

**(b) Substitution is an OPERATOR-RULED VARIANCE, not a written justification**
(`D-002`, adopted as strengthened). If harness evidence is unavailable or
immature at gate close, the item may be evidenced by another governed route —
but the substitution requires operator approval and is **recorded in the register
as a variance**, applying the same instrument as the threshold variance at §5.5.

Rev A permitted substitution on a justification recorded in the gate record.
DeepSeek dissented that this is a loophole: the justification criterion is not
objective, so the route can be taken by writing prose. The variance form removes
that by making substitution an act someone rules rather than an author's account
of why they did not use the harness.

**Gemini's contrary position is recorded, not resolved away.** Gemini's dissent
section says "None", explicitly endorsing preferred-primary as pragmatically
justified "provided the equivalence test (§7.2c) is strictly enforced". This was
the round's only genuine bench split and the chair resolved it toward the stronger
form. The contrary view stands in the record because the convention against
summarising dissents protects it most when the chair rules the other way.

**(c) Equivalence test.** A substitute qualifies only if it meets, by its own
mechanism, the properties that make a Regime-2 run citable: produced under a
governed act; cited as path @ commit SHA or a CI result transcribed as text,
never a bare path and never `main` (`docs/build/B7_GATE.md` §3); reproducible or
immutable by construction; and asserting the same proposition as the scenario it
replaces, rather than a weaker one nearby.

### 7.3 Mapping to the B7 criteria as they actually read

`docs/build/B7_GATE.md` §5 carries a list headed "Not allowed — absence blocks
the gate regardless of what else landed", stating that the list "is exhaustive
as written and is the required-evidence list at §3". Its five items are quoted
verbatim and mapped; the gate document's fuller descriptions are not paraphrased
here, because a plan that paraphrases a gate's required-evidence list is how two
accounts of one requirement begin.

|---|---|
| corpus manifest with provenance, licence status, SA5 classification and time-versioned eligibility | **Not harness evidence.** A document artefact under `DEC-0014`; a run pins the snapshot, it does not evidence the fields. |
| grounding test | **Harness-primary.** Citation-following class (§3.1). |
| negative test | **Harness-primary.** Gap-detection class (§3.1). |

**Stated plainly rather than mapped over:** `docs/build/B7_GATE.md` contains no

**The operational implication, stated in terms because Rev A routed the silence

it means the harness is not *required* by the gate, not that it is excluded.

**A gate-synchronisation patch is OWED before first gate exit under Regime 2**

The requirement is recorded at the B7-exit ruling pack, and its owner is the

plan cannot discharge by drafting. Adding D1/D2 to B7's required list would be a
change to gate criteria, which is not this plan's to make.

### 7.4 The admissibility bound

Only Regime 2 runs meeting their entry criteria at ruled spec versions are
citable as gate evidence; the reproducibility identity is mandatory; and a run
with a failed or skipped pre-flight is inadmissible regardless of outcome
(`TOR §5A:99`). Regime 1 results are inadmissible permanently and cannot be
promoted (`TOR §5A:85`). Preferred-primary never softens admissibility: it
governs which admissible route is preferred, not what counts as admissible.

Reviewer B recommended a hard requirement, Regime-2 evidence mandatory with

no single-point dependency; Reviewer A took no position and required this Test
Plan to make the relationship precise (rulings record, line 44). The operator
adopted C's position. This section is what satisfies Reviewer A's requirement.

### 7.6 Sufficiency

Which scenarios constitute sufficient harness evidence for a B7 exit item is
determined by the coverage obligations at §2.1 and the acceptance criteria at §5,
bounded by the PROVISIONAL typology label at §2.4. Invalidation of prior harness
evidence by corpus, model, environment or evaluator movement is §9.

## 8. The formal-execution governance pack

This is the section whose absence blocks Regime 2 (`TOR §5A:93`), and it is a
first-class section rather than an appendix by requirement (`TOR §4:49`). Seven
elements, enumerated at `TOR §5A:94-100`.

### 8.1 Entry criteria per session

All six must hold (`TOR §5A:94`). **Every criterion names its assertion surface
or names its deferral** — "machine-asserted" appears nowhere in this plan without
one or the other (`F-GROK-07`). Criteria 2 to 6 are machine-asserted under
Regime 2 per H-10, which ruled that the run-record obligations that were human
records under Regime 1 are asserted by the instrument under Regime 2.

| # | Criterion | Assertion surface | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Environment asserted via pre-flight, **including environment identity** | `scripts/d22a_preflight.py`, exit code | Non-zero exit; UNKNOWN and SKIPPED both exit non-zero |
| 2 | Corpus at a listed snapshot, post listing-act pin | **DEFERRED to the runner build** — instrument not yet named | Deferral stated; not asserted today |
| 3 | Spec versions ruled, not merely valid | **DEFERRED to the runner build** — instrument not yet named | Deferral stated; not asserted today |
| 4 | Model version stated **and resolving to an immutable content-addressed artefact** | **DEFERRED to the runner build** — DVC resolution per §6.8 | Deferral stated; not asserted today |
| 5 | No unconsumed suspension in force, per §8.3 | **DEFERRED to the runner build** — ledger query | Deferral stated; not asserted today |
| 6 | Golden-fixture suite GREEN, per §5.7(a) | Test-suite exit code | Non-zero exit blocks the session |

**Four of six are deferrals and that is stated rather than smoothed over.**
Rev A declared criteria 2 to 5 machine-asserted while naming no instrument,
command or failure mode for any of them; the sixth criterion added at Rev B would
have arrived in the same condition. Criterion 6 is named concretely because a test
suite already has an exit code and there was no reason to leave it unnamed.
Criteria 2 to 5 are honestly recorded as owed by the runner build, and each is a
named deferral rather than an unexamined claim.

**Pre-flight failure fails hard** (`amendment 7`): non-zero exit immediately,
blocking all downstream steps, so no partial or corrupted ledger entry can be
written in CI or locally. A session that cannot assert its environment does not
produce a weaker result; it produces no result.

**Criterion 1 is extended at Rev B to cover environment identity**, which is what
makes §9.2 row 6 assertable rather than aspirational. Pre-flight pins and verifies
the environment identity defined at §9.5; the extension is a runner-build
obligation recorded at Appendix B. **An environment-movement invalidation row
that no instrument can evaluate would restate the §6.5 problem in a new place — a
rule whose trigger nobody can observe** — so the extension is the row's
assertability condition rather than a convenience attached to it.

**Considered and not carried:** a distinct dry-run pipeline mode relaxing the hard
failure on SKIPPED and UNKNOWN states, on the argument that it impedes CI
diagnostics and developer velocity during early iteration (`G-ALT-CI`). The
two-regime design already provides that mode: Regime 1 exists for diagnostics and
iteration without evidential weight, and its results are permanently inadmissible
by that same design. A diagnostics mode inside the formal regime would reintroduce
the soft-pass, and UNKNOWN never collapsing to green is the founding rule. The
velocity concern is legitimate and is served by the regime boundary, not by
weakening the gate.

### 8.2 Exit criteria per session

1. **Every scheduled scenario executed, or its non-execution recorded with a
   reason** (`TOR §5A:95`). A scenario silently absent from a results set is
   indistinguishable from one never scheduled.
2. **Results ledgered**, each entry carrying its regime marker (`TOR §4:61`),
   which is mechanically immutable per H-9 — "a marker a process can edit is a
   promotion path".
3. **Acceptance verdicts recorded** per §5.6, in the artefact and the ledger,
   immutable by the same mechanism as the marker, with the ledger entry carrying
   the content-addressed artefact's hash per §5.7(b).
4. **A summary produced.**

### 8.3 Suspension, halted sessions, and resumption

**A session halts on** (`TOR §5A:96`): pre-flight red mid-run; environment
drift; corpus or model version change mid-session.

**A halted session's partial results are recorded, not discarded** (`F-DS-07`).
Rev A's §9.3 covered invalidated results and said nothing about a session stopped
mid-flight, leaving it open whether partial results were kept, marked or thrown
away.

> Partial results from a halted session are recorded in the ledger with
> `session_status: halted` and the reason for the halt. **They are inadmissible
> for any purpose other than diagnosing the suspension.** Re-entry is a separate
> session entry carrying its own pinned reproducibility identity.

**Re-entry requires** a fresh pre-flight and a re-pinned reproducibility identity.
Re-entry is not resumption: the identity has been re-pinned, so results before and
after were produced under different pinned conditions and must not be presented
as one continuous set. Which prior results survive the movement that caused the
suspension is determined by §9.

A suspension is *consumed* when its re-entry conditions have been met and
recorded. An unconsumed suspension blocks session entry under §8.1 criterion 5.

### 8.4 Session objectives and scope

Each formal session names its scenario set and purpose **before execution**;
additions mid-session are a new session (`TOR §5A:97`).

The reason is admissibility rather than tidiness: a scenario added after results
have been seen is selected in knowledge of what the suite was already producing,
and the resulting set cannot be distinguished from one assembled to support a
conclusion. Naming the set in advance is what makes the session a test rather
than a search. It is the same discipline §5.2 criterion 4 applies to the expected
set.

### 8.5 Defect classification and routing

At §10, which is a pack element and not a separate concern (`TOR §5A:98`).

### 8.6 Evidence admissibility

At §7.4. Two consequences worth stating because they run in the unfavourable
direction: a **passing** run with a skipped pre-flight is inadmissible, because
the conditions under which the pass was obtained were never asserted; and a
Regime 1 result that happens to be excellent is inadmissible permanently and is
re-run under Regime 2 if wanted, never promoted.

### 8.7 Re-test and regression rules

At §9, stated operationally as `amendment 1` requires.

## 9. Invalidation and re-test rules

### 9.1 Two grounds for invalidation, one consequence

**Results are invalidated on two different grounds, and §9.2 covers both.**

- **Movement in a pinned leg** invalidates only those results whose claim depends
  on that leg. The alternative — any movement invalidating everything — would
  discard the separation `DEC-0014` item 3 was bought to provide, and would
  over-invalidate so obviously that it would be worked around rather than
  followed.
- **A confirmed defect in the instrument** invalidates the results the defective
  instrument produced. **This is not a movement in a pinned leg**: nothing in the
  reproducibility identity changed, and the instrument was wrong all along.

Rev A stated only the first and called it the section's rule. The harness-defect
row adopted at `F-DS-03` does not fit that rule, and adding it without restating
the opening would leave the section asserting a principle its own table breaks.
Both grounds carry the same consequence — the affected results are invalidated,
marked, and re-run if wanted — and they are distinguished because they are found
by different routes: a movement is observed, a defect is discovered.

### 9.2 The table

| # | Ground | Invalidates | Survives |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Eligible-set hash changes | All retrieval-class results | Scoring-class results not pinned to the corpus |
| 2 | Retrieval-snapshot hash changes, eligible-set hash unchanged — **or any change to recorded processing facts per §6.4 where those facts are present** | All retrieval-class results — processing drift changes what the retriever sees | The same-documents claim, which the eligible-set hash still evidences |
| 3 | Manifest hash changes, both content hashes unchanged | Nothing | All results. The change is metadata-only; it is recorded, not acted on |
| 4 | Model version changes | All results produced with the superseded model | Results at other model versions |
| 5 | Spec hash changes | That scenario's prior results | Every other scenario |
| 6 | **Environment/toolchain identity changes** per §9.5 | All results produced under the superseded environment identity | Results whose claim does not rest on execution — none, in practice, for retrieval-class |
| 7 | **Evaluator version changes**, no defect confirmed | Nothing by itself, provided the golden-fixture suite is GREEN at both versions and the fact is recorded | All results. The version change is recorded in the identity and is visible, which is its purpose |
| 8 | **Harness defect affecting scoring or acceptance evaluation, CONFIRMED** | All results produced by the affected evaluator version for the affected scenario classes | Results from unaffected evaluator versions and unaffected classes |

**Row 2 is marked PROVISIONAL** until the corpus-side processing obligation
closes, for the reason §9.4 gives. **Row 3 STANDS and its reachable cases are
enumerated at §9.3.** Rows 6, 7 and 8 are new at Rev B.

**Row 7 is the one row whose default is "nothing", and the reasoning is stated
because that looks permissive.** The evaluator version joins the reproducibility
identity so that a change is *visible*, which is the ruling's stated purpose — not
so that every refactor invalidates the record. What makes visibility sufficient
is §5.7(a): the golden-fixture suite asserts the threshold comparison at known
inputs with known verdicts, including boundary cases. An evaluator version that is
green against the same fixtures computes the same verdicts, and invalidating on
that basis alone would impose re-runs to reprove something already asserted. Where
the fixture suite is **not** green at both versions, or where a behaviour change
is intended, the change is a confirmed defect or a deliberate semantic change and
falls to row 8. **Rows 7 and 8 are drafted as a pair and neither works without the
fixture suite.**

### 9.3 Row 3 is reachable, and these are the cases

Rev A's row 3 was challenged as unreachable under the plan's own immutability
rules (`F-DS-05`). **The row is reachable; the unreachability argument rests on a
false premise.** It reasons that a manifest version change implies the identity
block changed and therefore the content hashes changed. That conflates a
document's `content_sha256` — which hashes the document's **content** — with a
hash over its manifest entry. Appending an eligibility transition, or bumping the
version label, changes the manifest entry and leaves the document's content hash
untouched.

Verified against the live manifest at `manifest_version` 2026-08-13.8 on
2026-08-14, using the platform's own hash functions and mutating only in-memory
copies; independently re-verified on 2026-08-15 against the same unmoved manifest.
**Four of four probed cases reach the row**, each moving `manifest_sha256` while
both `eligible_set_sha256` and `retrieval_snapshot_sha256` hold:

1. **A `manifest_version` label bump alone.** The label is in the canonicalised
   manifest and in neither content hash.
2. **Processing facts filled in on a non-eligible document.** Both content hashes
   range over eligible documents only, so anything done to the fourteen at
   `pending_review` reaches this row and nothing else.
3. **An eligibility transition on a non-eligible document** — `pending_review` to
   `withdrawn`. Membership of the eligible set does not change, so no content
   hash moves.
4. **A re-ingest of an eligible document** producing the same chunker version,
   embedding model and chunk count, with a new `ingest_timestamp`.

Case 4 is why §6.4 defines recorded processing facts as three fields and not
four. If row 2 fired on `ingest_timestamp`, case 4 would invalidate every
retrieval-class result while nothing the retriever sees had changed.

### 9.4 A caveat that bounds row two

**Where processing facts are absent from the manifest, retrieval-snapshot
equality is not evidence of processing stability** — the hashes match because
both sides folded in the same absent values (§6.5). Results pinned over absent
processing facts cannot rely on row 2 of the table above, which is why that row
carries a PROVISIONAL marker.

This is stated, not ruled. It is presently latent: as of `manifest_version`
2026-08-13.8 every eligible document carries a complete processing block. It
becomes live the moment an eligible document does not, and it is the strongest
practical argument for closing the corpus-side processing obligation.

### 9.5 What constitutes an environment identity change

Row 6 needs a definition or it is a rule whose trigger nobody can observe
(`F-CHATGPT-04` with `F-GEMINI-03`, adopted jointly). **The environment identity
comprises four elements, and a change in any of them is a movement:**

1. **Runner dependency versions** — the resolved versions of the packages the
   runner executes under.
2. **Runtime** — the Python version and the interpreter identity, as
   `scripts/d22a_preflight.py` already reports at its environment-identity
   assertion.
3. **Vector-index configuration** — index type and parameters, and the identity
   of the store the retrieval runs against.
4. **Embedder configuration as distinct from model version** — normalisation,
   distance metric, and any parameter affecting how the pinned model's output is
   used.

Element 4 is the one Rev A had no way to express at all: a distance-metric change
leaves the model version, both content hashes and the spec hash untouched while
changing every ranking the retriever produces. The model-version leg of §6.8 does
not cover it, which is why it is named separately here.

### 9.6 What must re-run

A result invalidated on either ground re-runs **under Regime 2 at the new pin**,
as a fresh session under §8.4 with its scenario set named in advance.
Invalidation does not delete the prior result: it remains in the ledger, marked,
with the movement or the confirmed defect that invalidated it recorded against
it. A ledger from which invalidated results disappear cannot show what changed.

Routing a defect under §10 and invalidating its products under §9 are **distinct,
connected acts**. Routing does not invalidate; invalidation does not close the
defect.

## 10. Defect classification and routing

`TOR §5A:98`: "harness defects to WS-E ledger; system-under-test findings to CL
register or the relevant build-stage evidence; corpus defects (a scenario
failing because the corpus is wrong) to the corpus rulings process. **One
defect, one home.**"

### 10.1 The three classes

| Class | What it is | Home |
|---|---|---|
| **Harness defect** | The instrument is wrong: runner, schema, pre-flight, ledger, scoring implementation, evaluation logic. The result says nothing about the system under test. | `docs/governance/WS-E_INCIDENTS.md` |
| **System-under-test finding** | The instrument worked and the system behaved as reported. | CL register, or the relevant build-stage evidence |
| **Corpus defect** | The scenario failed because the corpus is wrong — a missing document, a wrong eligibility state, a bad extract. | The corpus rulings process |

### 10.2 Why one defect gets one home

A defect filed in two registers is counted twice and closed once. More
importantly the three registers carry different obligations: a WS-E entry
records an incident about the evidence chain, a CL entry records a change owed
to the system, and a corpus ruling is a governed act against an append-only
store. Filing a corpus defect as a harness defect would put a corpus correction
outside corpus governance entirely — the route by which an append-only store
acquires an unrecorded change.

### 10.3 Classifying is a step, and the default is not "harness"

The classification is not obvious from the failure. A retrieval scenario
returning the wrong documents may be a scoring implementation that computes
recall wrongly (harness), a retriever that ranks badly (system-under-test), or a
corpus in which the expected grounding document is mis-chunked or wrongly
ineligible (corpus).

**Where the classification is genuinely unclear, the finding is recorded as
unclassified with its candidates named**, and classified by ruling. An
unclassified finding is a known state; a finding filed in the wrong register is
a lost one. F1 is the worked example and is so recorded at §12.

### 10.4 What routing does not do

Routing a defect is not closing it, and a routed defect does not by itself
invalidate the run it arose in. Whether a defect of a given class invalidates
prior results is determined by §9 — and for a confirmed evaluation-logic defect,
by §9.2 row 8 specifically.

**One routing order is fixed rather than left to judgment:** a failure on a
scenario carrying the `confound: single_chunk` marker routes to the §12.3(3)
investigation **before** defect classification. Classifying first would force a
choice between corpus, harness and system-under-test while the known confound is
precisely what makes that choice unsafe.

## 11. Migration-diff comparison semantics (reference)

### 11.1 Reference, not definition

`amendment 3` rules that the semantics are settled per scenario and "live in the
schema so each scenario declares its own comparison rule". The normative
statement is the `migration_diff` definition in
`arcaai/harness/schema/scenario_spec_v0.2.schema.json`; this plan points at it.

The reason is not deference. A rule stated in two places drifts, and the schema
is executable while prose here is not — so a divergence would surface as a
validation failure against a plan a reviewer had already read and believed.

### 11.2 The mechanism as the schema implements it

- `migration_diff` is required on every spec, of either class.
- `comparison` is `bit_identical` or `tolerance`.
- A tolerance comparison requires a tolerance block, itself requiring at minimum
  a metric and a maximum absolute delta.
- A bit-identical comparison carrying a tolerance block is **rejected**: such a
  spec "is a contradiction, and is rejected rather than resolved by precedence."
  Resolving by precedence would silently pick one of two things the author
  asserted.

### 11.3 Why the tolerance must name its cause

Naming the nondeterministic component "is not optional prose: it is how a
reviewer checks that a tolerance was chosen rather than reached for". A bare
delta is unfalsifiable at review; a delta attributed to a named component can be
argued with.

**Finding recorded, not resolved here:** the nondeterminism-source field is
described as the reviewer's check but is **not** in the tolerance block's
required list, which carries only the metric and the maximum absolute delta. A
schema-valid tolerance can therefore omit its cause. The fix is a schema change
and hence a new versioned file; it is queued as a v0.3 candidate. Until then,
this plan requires a tolerance without a stated cause to be treated as
incomplete at review under §5.5's variance rule.

### 11.4 Why per-scenario

The schema gives the argument: the pre-migration baseline compares results
across an environment move, "and a result whose comparison rule was never
declared cannot be diffed without inventing one after the fact." An invented
rule is chosen with the diff already in hand — the point at which the choice
stops being a control.

Which scenarios take which rule is a per-scenario authoring decision under the
coverage obligations at §2.1. Whether an environment move invalidates prior
results is §9.2 row 6.

### 11.5 Gate consequence

Per `Ruling 4`, the D2.6 pre-migration baseline is a formal hard precondition to
any serving-layer cutover to AWS, and the D3.5 diff report is formally reviewed
before migration is declared complete (rulings record line 48; `TOR §9:139-140`).
The comparison runs at identical spec hashes and corpus snapshot pre- and
post-cutover (`TOR §11`).

D2.6 is a Regime 2 run and requires the ruled Test Plan (`TOR §4:62`). **Until
this plan is ruled, the migration path is blocked at D2.6.**

## Appendix D — noticed during authoring, held for round 2

Recorded rather than fixed. Each is outside the round-1 warrant, and folding an
unforced improvement into Rev B would create fresh finding surface that round 2
has not seen (`TOR §5A:101` governs post-acceptance amendment).

1. **§2.2's series table is derived from `source:` prose by hand**, and Rev B
   inherits the derivation unchanged. §2.4 names the hazard; the table itself is
   still hand-maintained and unverified by any mechanical check.
2. **`RQA-107` covers four statute documents of very uneven chunk density** — 1,
   1, 7 and 16 chunks. Whether one scenario can meaningfully cover a series whose
   members differ sixteen-fold in chunk count is a coverage question this plan's
   obligations do not currently ask.
3. **The `top_k` ratio bound is stated against the eligible chunk count**, which
   is itself a manifest-derived figure with no mechanical assertion in the plan.
   The same class of hazard as item 1.
4. **No standards-mapping line is carried.** The convention is queued and its
   per-class content is defined once in the control framework, which does not yet
   exist; composing a mapping here rather than copying one would pre-empt it.

## Appendix E — drafting decisions put to the chair, and their rulings

Five points where Rev B drafting made a choice the disposition delegated but did
not settle. Each was flagged rather than presented as settled, so that an ADOPTED
row would not be mistaken at review for a closed question. **All five are now
ruled, on 2026-08-15**; none is outstanding.

**E1 — Obligation B: PER-SERIES. RULED 2026-08-15.**
Drafted first in a direction-based form — one scenario per structural kind of
edge, two in all — and flagged as the weaker of two defensible forms. The chair
ruled the stronger form: **every emitting series carries its own scenario, six in
all.** Rationale on the record: acceptance safety outweighs authoring cost, and a
knowingly weaker obligation invites a MATERIAL finding from a panel that has
already once forced this rule to be restated. The direction-based form is
WITHDRAWN. Implemented at §2.1 and Appendix A.2; the scenario count rises from
eleven to fifteen.

**E2 — The §5.2 expected-set criteria: ENDORSED AS DRAFTED, with one addition.
RULED 2026-08-15.**
The disposition required "criteria the plan states"; the plan stated none, so all
four at §5.2 were newly composed and none inherited. They were held for the
chair's reading rather than endorsed on their existence, since sight-unseen
endorsement would be delegation rather than confirmation. **All four are endorsed
unchanged.** One sentence is added: marginal inclusions or exclusions of documents
in E are noted with reasons at scenario acceptance. Rationale on the record — it
keeps the judgment human, makes the criterion-2 / criterion-3 tension auditable,
and avoids false precision. Implemented at §5.2.

**E3 — The `top_k` bound figure: 10% CONFIRMED as drafted. RULED 2026-08-15.**
The ratio form was ruled in the disposition; the figure was not. Ten per cent
permits `top_k` 7 at today's 71-chunk denominator and flags `top_k` 10.
Implemented at §5.4.

**E4 — §9.2 row 7 default: ENDORSED as drafted. RULED 2026-08-15.**
An evaluator version change invalidates nothing by itself, with the golden-fixture
suite as the confirming discriminator, and a behaviour change or confirmed defect
falling to row 8. Ruled consistent with the C3/C4 spine. Implemented at §9.2.

**E5 — The `R-1` four-candidate list: ENDORSED. RULED 2026-08-15.**
`R-1` reserved whether its own "no Rev B edit required" extended to the routed
consequences, one of which widens the §12.3(3) candidate list from three
mechanisms to four. **Ruled: the four-candidate list is the disposition carried,
not a fresh drafting choice.** Implemented at §12.3(3) and §12.4.
