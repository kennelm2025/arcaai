### R2-1 — the evaluator leg, and the hole two reviewers found from opposite sides

`F-GROK-08` and `R2-01` are not the same finding and must not be merged, but they
are two consequences of one act: carrying in the structural option at the Rev B
stop turned the reproducibility triple into a quadruple without re-reading what
the rest of the plan said about it.

**Grok found the language problem.** Every prior statement of "the triple" is now
wrong by omission, and neither a human reader nor a later instrument has anything
telling them so. **ChatGPT found the logic problem.** Rev B's own §9.2 says an
intended behaviour change "falls to row 8", but row 8 is explicitly a *confirmed
defect*, and an intentional semantic change is not a defect. So the case with the
greatest evidential consequence — the evaluator deliberately made to score
differently — was the one case with no route.

**Both fixes are drafted together**, because row 7's three-way split is what makes
the fourth leg mean anything, and the four-legged declaration is what makes the
split findable.

**Scope limit, ruled explicitly.** `TOR §5:74` and `TOR §8:124` both state the
identity as three legs plus a seed. **They are not amended here.** The TOR states a
floor; the plan adds to it. Amending TOR text is a `TOR §5A:101` post-acceptance
act and is recorded as owed rather than performed inside a Test Plan revision.

**On the fixture suite's reach**, which ChatGPT identified precisely: §5.7
describes the suite as asserting the *threshold comparison*, which is narrower
than demonstrating semantic equivalence of the whole evaluator. Rev C must
therefore not lean on the fixture suite to carry the equivalence claim in case 1
without saying what equivalence means. The equivalence criterion is drafted to
the distinction rather than borrowed from the fixture contract.

### R2-2 — `F-GEM-REG-01` ruled down from BLOCKING to MATERIAL

**This is the round's only severity adjudication, and the reasons are recorded in
full because the rating was a reviewer's and the change is the chair's.**

**Reason 1 — the exposure attaches to external reliance, not to internal
execution.** Gemini's analysis is about generating "formal gate evidence" on
unvalidated typology data and the liability that follows from asserting coverage.
That risk is real and is accepted. But it crystallises at the point where the
evidence is *relied upon by an external party* — a regulator, a client, a skilled
person — not at the point where a Regime 2 run executes internally against a
corpus whose limitations the plan itself states in terms.

**Reason 2 — the PROVISIONAL label was the ruled round-1 disposition, faithfully
carried.** `F-DS-04`'s composite point 2 required the typology gap to be **named,
not papered over**, and Rev B names it: the matrix is PROVISIONAL, and a Regime 2
run against the current manifest is stated not to be evidence of typology
coverage. Ruling the carried form BLOCKING would penalise Rev C for implementing
what round 1 ruled, which is the wrong signal to send about carriage.

**Reason 3 — the panel's three architecture reviewers did not re-find it.**
ChatGPT considered the point explicitly and declined to raise it, recording that
the provisional status is "intentional and faithfully carried, not an accidental
gap" and that "the round-1 ruling specifically required the gap to be named rather
than papered over, so I would not re-find it." DeepSeek tabulated the same
carriage as FAITHFUL. A regulatory reviewer seeing exposure the architecture
reviewers do not see is exactly what the lens was fielded for and is not
dismissed; but unanimity in the other direction bears on severity.

**What is adopted, and it is not the proposed fix.** Gemini required Regime 2
execution to be hard-gated on the manifest typology field landing. **That is not
adopted.** Gating execution on a corpus-workstream artefact would stop the harness
entirely on a dependency the Test Plan does not own, and the D2.6 migration
baseline depends on Regime 2 opening. **Adopted instead, and it addresses the
exposure at the point where it exists:** Rev C states in terms that **Regime-2
evidence may not be cited externally — to any regulator, client or audit — as
evidence of typology coverage** until the manifest typology field and the
controlled vocabulary land. Internal execution proceeds; external reliance does
not. The typology field becomes a **named pre-B7-exit owed item**, so the
dependency is tracked where B7 exit is ruled rather than left to the corpus
workstream's own cadence.

**Recorded honestly:** this is a chair ruling against a fielded reviewer's stated
severity and stated remedy, in the round where that reviewer's lens was the reason
the seat existed. The finding is sustained, not rejected, and its substance
changes the plan.

### R2-3 — `F-DS-10`, coverage and precision made to compose

DeepSeek's finding is that Rev B is internally coherent on each rule and does not
ensure the rules compose: coverage is satisfiable by binary probes over every
series, complete by the plan's own criteria and weak as evidence.

**The reviewer's own proposed sentence is adopted verbatim in substance**, rather
than redrafted, because it states the minimum that closes the hole without
enlarging the coverage obligation: a series covered only by binary probes requires
at least one `|E| ≥ 5` scenario **or** a stated justification why a binary probe
suffices for that series.

**The alternative the reviewer offered is not adopted** — redefining coverage as
"at least one scenario that, if it passed, would be citable as evidence of
retrieval quality under §5.4". It is the more elegant form and it moves more: it
would make the coverage rule depend on the acceptance rule, and a change to §5.4
would then silently change what coverage means. The adopted form keeps the two
rules separate and makes their interaction explicit, which is the property the
finding was actually about.

### R2-4 — `F-GROK-09`, and why ownership is the operator's

The fixture contract is drafted as Grok specified. **Ownership is ruled to the
OPERATOR rather than the runner build**, which is the choice worth explaining: the
suite asserts the threshold comparison, and the thresholds are governed by §5,
whose departures are ruled variances requiring operator approval. Assigning the
suite to the build would put the artefact that checks the rule under a different
owner from the rule, and the refresh obligation would sit with whoever noticed
rather than with whoever ruled.

The refresh rule follows from that: **when a §5 threshold moves, the fixture suite
is refreshed as part of the same act**, not as a follow-on task.

### R2-5 — the composite, and why two ratings become one

`F-DS-11` and `F-GEM-REG-04` are the same hole approached from opposite ends.
DeepSeek asks what §9.5 element 3 *means* and finds it unassertable; Gemini asks
what pre-flight *does* and finds nothing pinning it. Neither is complete alone: a
tightened definition nobody asserts is still aspirational, and an asserted hash
over an undefined scope pins the wrong thing.

**Rated MATERIAL, the higher of the two**, because a composite takes the severity
its combined substance warrants and Gemini's MINOR rating was made against a
narrower reading of the same defect.

**The named-list mechanism is what makes both halves work.** A list of material
parameters, maintained by the runner build and extensible, with **the list's own
hash carried in the environment identity** — so that changing what counts as
material is itself a detectable movement. Without that last element the list
becomes a place to quietly narrow the definition.

### R2-6 — `F-GEM-REG-02`, absolute cap alongside the ratio

Rev B chose a ratio deliberately, and the reasoning was that an absolute bound
silently loosens as the corpus grows. **Gemini's finding is that the ratio has the
same defect in the other direction** — it silently loosens the *absolute* window,
so a scenario's noise payload can grow tenfold while remaining compliant.

Both are right, which is why the ruling keeps both: **the ratio bounds the
proportion, the per-class absolute cap bounds the window, and a corpus expansion
that would push a scenario past its recorded cap requires a ruled variance.** The
variance is the model-risk re-validation event Gemini asked for, expressed in the
instrument this plan already has rather than a new one.

### R2-7 — `F-GEM-REG-03`, and why the signature is not adopted

The machine-readable `invalidation_status` field and the mandatory filtering are
adopted in full. **The cryptographic revocation signature is not, and the reason is
recorded because refusing part of a regulatory reviewer's remedy needs one.**

A revocation signature answers the question *who has the authority to invalidate,
and can that be proved to a party who does not trust the ledger's operator*. On a
single-operator ledger, in a repository whose audit store is append-only by
database grant and whose commits are not yet key-bound, the signature would be
generated and verified by the same party, which is a ceremony rather than a
control. **It becomes a real control at the point the repository has cryptographic
signing at all** — which is a named mechanism on the audit-readiness item and not
this plan's to invent.

**What the concern actually required is fully met:** the supervisory exposure was
that an invalidated run could be *read as active* by a gate script or audit
export. A machine-readable flag plus mandatory filtering closes exactly that, and
closes it in a form the runner build can assert.
