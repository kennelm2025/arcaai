### R-1 — `DS-CURRENCY`, embedder currency. Ruled 2026-08-14

**Ruling: NOTED.** The observation is out of Test Plan scope, as the reviewer
himself rated it — he declined to raise it as a finding on the ground that the
plan's scope is the test plan, not the retrieval architecture selection. **No
RevB edit is required by the observation.** It is nonetheless **ROUTED**, with
three consequences.

**Consequence 1 — a decision-record act is QUEUED.**
The embedder selection, `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, has no DEC or ADR defending it and
names no upgrade triggers. It is a load-bearing choice that entered the platform
as a fact rather than as a decision. The act is to author the decision record —
defending the choice or replacing it — with trigger criteria named.
*Owner: platform architecture (operator). DEC-shaped. It consumes a register
number only when ruled, not when queued.*

**Consequence 2 — timing note, recorded verbatim.**

> The §9.2 model-version invalidation row makes an embedder change free TODAY
> (zero Regime 2 results exist) and increasingly expensive with every formal
> baseline accumulated. The decision record is therefore recommended BEFORE
> Regime 2 opens.

The mechanism is the plan's own: the invalidation table rules that a model
version change invalidates all results produced with that model. With no Regime
2 results in existence the row costs nothing to trigger. Every formal baseline
accumulated after that raises the price of the same decision, and the price is
paid in re-runs rather than in argument.

**Consequence 3 — cross-reference to the §12.3 named investigation.**
The single-chunk investigation gains **embedder choice** as a fourth candidate
mechanism, alongside corpus design, chunker parameters and retrieval
configuration. The two questions interlock: whether single-chunk statute
extracts are structurally disadvantaged is not separable from which embedding
model is doing the ranking, and an investigation that fixed the embedder as
given would be assuming away one of its own candidates.

**Standards tie-in — M13.** Model-currency review is standards-register
material when M13 lands, carrying the PRA SS1/23 model-risk shape: a model in
use without a recorded justification, and without stated criteria for when it
should be replaced, is the shape a model-risk reviewer asks about first.

**One scope question raised by this ruling, for the chair rather than resolved
here.** The ruling states that no RevB edit is required. Consequence 3 adds a
fourth candidate mechanism to the investigation whose candidate list is written
into the plan text at §12.3 and §12.4, which reads as a RevB edit. The two are
reconcilable if "no RevB edit required" is scoped to the observation itself
rather than to the routed consequences — which is the natural reading — but it
is recorded rather than assumed, because the failure mode is RevB shipping with
a three-candidate list that this ruling has already widened to four.

### R-2 — cluster C1, coverage and typology. Ruled 2026-08-14

The composite fix is recorded in full in the `F-DS-04` row and is not restated
here. This entry carries what does not fit a cell.

**What "not accepted as RevA" means.** Round 1 does not close in acceptance.
RevB is authored carrying the composite, and the panel's round-1 outcome is a
disposition, not a ruling that the plan is fit. Regime 2 remains shut, and with
it D2.6 and the serving-layer cutover that depends on it.

**Why BLOCKING converted rather than being overturned.** The severity was not
disputed. It converts because the fix is specified and bounded — five named
points with owners — rather than because the finding was found to be smaller
than rated. A BLOCKING finding with an unspecified fix would not convert.

**Subsumption is a discharge relationship, and it runs one way.** Four findings
are discharged BY the composite and not independently: `F-GROK-01`,
`F-CHATGPT-01`, its dissent, and `F-GEMINI-01`. RevB satisfying the composite
discharges all four. **If the composite is later narrowed, the subsumed findings
revive** — they are not closed on their own terms and their severities stand
behind the composite rather than being retired by it.

**A schema finding that changes what point 3 costs, verified rather than
assumed.** Point 3 requires each scenario spec to declare the fraud typology it
covers. `arcaai/harness/schema/scenario_spec_v0.2.schema.json` **already carries
`typology` as a REQUIRED top-level field** — string, minimum length 1, in the
required array alongside `scenario_class` and `vertical`. So point 3 needs no
schema change and no new version: the field exists and every valid spec already
declares it.

What point 3 actually needs is the two things the field does not supply:

1. **The plan naming that field as the typology-coverage mechanism**, which is a
   RevB sentence rather than an engineering act.
2. **A controlled vocabulary, or coverage cannot be computed.** The schema
   describes `typology` as "carried as an opaque string for the same reason as
   `vertical`" — the ADR-0009 boundary, which bars platform machinery from
   encoding vertical semantics. An opaque string means two specs may write
   `card-not-present` and `CNP fraud` and a coverage check will see two
   typologies where there is one, or miss a match where there is one. Point 3's
   assessability rests on that vocabulary existing.

**And the vocabulary cannot live in the schema without breaching ADR-0009.** The
opacity is deliberate and architectural, not an oversight. So the controlled
vocabulary belongs vertical-side — in this plan, or in a fraud-vertical
artefact — and RevB should say which. Recorded here because the natural
instinct on reading point 3 is to add an enum to the schema, and that is the one
place it must not go.

**Point 5 remains the real dependency.** Per-scenario declaration makes typology
coverage assessable at spec level; it does not make it assessable against the
corpus, because nothing connects a declared typology to the documents that
ground it until the manifest carries the field. Point 2's PROVISIONAL label is
therefore load-bearing and is not lifted by points 3 and 4.

### R-3 — cluster C2, threshold and acceptance. Ruled 2026-08-14

All six findings adopted. **§5 is rebuilt rather than patched**: six adopted
obligations land in one section, and a section amended six times by hand is how
a rule and its worked examples drift apart.

**F-DS-02's tail — the ALREADY-COVERED resolution, verified rather than
asserted.** The finding's concern was that a threshold adjusted between runs
would leave prior results standing while the acceptance bar moved. It does not:
the threshold lives in the spec file, the runner computes `spec_sha` over the
raw spec bytes, and §9.2's fifth row invalidates that scenario's prior results
on a spec-hash change. So any threshold edit — including a variance — already
invalidates what preceded it. Recorded in the row rather than left implicit,
because a control that works by accident is one refactor away from not working.

**GROK-02 and DS-01 share a boundary, and it is exactly `|E| = 5`.** The default
`ceil(0.8 × |E|) / |E|` returns 1.0 at `|E|` = 1, 2, 3 and 4, and first returns
a non-unit value — 0.8 — at `|E| = 5`. So GROK-02's binary-probe rule covers
`|E| ≤ 4` and the chair's steer for DS-01's precision boundary begins at
`|E| ≥ 5`: the two rules partition the range at the same point, with no gap and
no overlap. **That coincidence is load-bearing for RevB drafting.** If the
author moves one boundary without moving the other, the result is either a band
of scenarios that are binary and carry no precision obligation, or one that is
graded and carries two. The two rules should be drafted together and their
shared boundary stated once.

**GEMINI-02's denominator, computed from the live manifest.** At
`manifest_version` 2026-08-13.8 the eligible set is 16 documents totalling
**71 indexed chunks**, every document carrying a chunk count. Against that
denominator:

| `top_k` | Share of the eligible corpus |
|---|---|
| 5 | 7.0% |
| 10 | 14.1% |
| 20 | 28.2% |

Gemini's illustrative `top_k = 20` is therefore not hypothetical — it would
return more than a quarter of the entire corpus and pass any binary probe
trivially. **This argues for expressing the bound as a ratio rather than an
absolute**, because the denominator is going to move: 30 documents are listed
and 14 sit at `pending_review` awaiting the inclusion decision, so a `top_k`
that is 28% of the corpus today could be 15% after inclusion without anyone
having changed it. An absolute bound would silently loosen; a ratio holds.

Two figures worth keeping beside each other: 71 chunks is also the figure the
F1 commissioning record states independently, at a different manifest version
with the same 16 eligible documents. The two agree.

**Two obligations return for the chair rather than closing here.** DS-01's
precision boundary is delegated to RevB drafting with a steer, and CHATGPT-02's
expected-set criteria are described as "criteria the plan states" — which the
plan does not yet state. Both are drafting acts whose output is a rule, not
prose, so both return for the chair's word at the RevB stop. Recorded so that
neither is mistaken at RevB review for a settled matter merely because its row
reads ADOPTED.

### R-4 — cluster C3, evaluator and verdict integrity. Ruled 2026-08-14

All three adopted. Together they close the evaluator triangle, and the framing
is the point: **verified before use** (`F-GROK-03`), **tamper-evident in
output** (`F-CHATGPT-03`), **governed in failure** (`F-DS-03`). None of the
three closes the loop alone. Fixtures without content-addressing prove a correct
evaluator wrote a verdict nobody can bind to its inputs; content-addressing
without fixtures binds a verdict tightly to bytes that may be wrong; and both
without an invalidation route leave a confirmed defect with no consequence.

**`F-GROK-03` widens the §8.1 pack to six criteria, and that meets an open
finding.** The fixture-suite-green criterion joins the five at §8.1, and under
H-10 every Regime 2 entry criterion is machine-asserted. But `F-GROK-07` — still
open at the time of this ruling — records that criteria 2 to 5 are *declared*
machine-asserted with no instrument, command or failure mode named. **Adopting
`F-GROK-03` adds a sixth criterion to that unnamed set rather than to the
asserted one.** The fixture suite is the easiest of the six to assert concretely,
since a test suite already has an exit code, and RevB should not leave it in the
same condition as the four it joins. Recorded so the two are drafted together.

**`F-CHATGPT-03` invents nothing, and that claim is checked rather than
asserted.** Content-addressing is already this repository's ruled identity
mechanism: `DEC-0014` rejects a `git_commit` column outright on the ground that
"a manifest's identity is its content hash; the commit is how a human finds the
content, not what it is", and names the replay chain ending in a
content-addressed lookup. The runner applies the same pattern already, computing
`spec_sha` over raw spec bytes. Applying it to the D3.1 artefact is therefore an
application of a ruled pattern, not a new mechanism — which is what makes the
adoption cheap.

**`F-DS-03` changes what §9 IS, and RevB must say so.** §9 currently opens by
stating that the rule is leg-scoped: "movement in a pinned leg invalidates only
those results whose claim depends on that leg." **A confirmed harness defect is
not a movement in a pinned leg.** Nothing in the reproducibility identity has
changed; the instrument was wrong all along. So the new row does not fit the
section's own stated principle, and adding it without amending that opening
sentence would leave §9 asserting a rule its own table breaks. RevB restates the
section as covering **movements and confirmed defects**, which are two different
grounds for invalidation with one consequence.

**Scope note on the structural option now noted for the author.** Making
evaluation logic a separately-versioned component whose version joins the
reproducibility identity would turn the triple into a quadruple. That reaches
§6.7, the result artefact schema, and every prior statement of the triple in the
plan and in the TOR — `TOR §5:74` and `§8:124` both state it as three legs plus
a seed. It is a coherent proposal and a wide one; it is noted, not ruled, and it
returns at the RevB stop with that reach understood.

**`DS-PROTOCOL-02`, resolved as moot.** DeepSeek's closing claim of "two
BLOCKING gaps" against one BLOCKING rating has no disposition consequence now
that `F-DS-03`'s substance is fully adopted — the finding gets the treatment its
author's prose argued for, whatever its rating field said. The rated severity
governs the table, and the discrepancy stays in the record rather than being
tidied out of it.

### R-5 — cluster C4, invalidation completeness. Ruled 2026-08-14

**The environment row makes §9.2 six rows and closes the state machine.**
`F-CHATGPT-04` and `F-GEMINI-03` are adopted jointly because they are the same
hole seen from two sides: one reviewer found it by walking from §8.3's
suspension trigger to a §9 that had no matching movement, the other by asking
what happens when the runtime moves under an unchanged snapshot. The row's scope
is the union of both: runner dependency updates, runtime changes, vector-index
configuration, and embedder configuration as distinct from model version.

**Pre-flight extension is what makes the row real.** An environment-movement row
that no instrument can evaluate would restate the §9.4 problem in a new place —
a rule whose trigger nobody can observe. Extending pre-flight to pin and verify

#### The `F-DS-05` verification act, discharged

The chair deferred `F-DS-05` for verification with the warning that disposing a
consistency claim without re-reading the artefact would be the report-over-
artefact failure this repository exists to prevent. The act was performed on
2026-08-14 against the live manifest at `manifest_version` 2026-08-13.8, using
the platform's own hash functions, mutating only in-memory copies.

**Result: THE ROW IS REACHABLE. Four of four probed cases reach it** — each
moves `manifest_sha256` while both `eligible_set_sha256` and
`retrieval_snapshot_sha256` hold:

1. **A `manifest_version` label bump alone.** The label is in the canonicalised
   manifest and in neither content hash.
2. **Processing facts filled in on a NON-eligible document.** Both content
   hashes range over eligible documents only, so anything done to the fourteen
   at `pending_review` reaches this row and nothing else.
3. **An eligibility transition on a non-eligible document** — `pending_review`
   to `withdrawn`. Membership of the eligible set does not change, so no
   content hash moves.
4. **A re-ingest of an eligible document** producing the same chunker version,
   embedding model and chunk count, with a new `ingest_timestamp`.

**Where the unreachability argument goes wrong.** `F-DS-05` reasons that "the
manifest version changed, so the identity block changed, and the content hashes
changed". That conflates a document's `content_sha256` — which hashes the
document's CONTENT — with a hash over its manifest entry. Appending an
eligibility transition, or bumping the version label, changes the manifest entry
and leaves the document's content hash untouched. The finding's second limb, on
the identity/eligibility split, is a real drafting question and is not
disconfirmed by this; only the unreachability claim is.

**A drafting constraint this verification hands to `F-GROK-04`, and the two
must be read together.** Case 4 shows that `ingest_timestamp` is a recorded
processing observation that `retrieval_snapshot_sha256` does **not** cover — the
hash folds in `chunker_version`, `embedding_model` and `chunk_count` only, three
of the four observations `DEC-0014` names. That exclusion is correct, since a
timestamp would make every re-ingest look like processing drift. But `F-GROK-04`
strengthens row two to fire "on any change to recorded processing facts", and if
RevB drafts that phrase literally it will fire on a re-ingest timestamp and
invalidate retrieval results for no reason. **"Recorded processing facts" must
be defined in RevB as the three the hash covers, not the four the manifest
carries.** Two adopted findings in one cluster, and their wording collides
unless drafted together.

### R-6 — singletons and pairs. Ruled 2026-08-14

**The F1 bar, ruled explicitly because a dissent asked for it.** D-001 asked the
