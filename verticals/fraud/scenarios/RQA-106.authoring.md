## Series and query

Typology. Two eligible documents: `SYN-TY-01` (4 chunks) and `SYN-TY-02` (4
chunks), a 1.0x ratio, so no density-stratified note is owed.

> Can the same customer be both a victim of authorised push payment fraud and a
> money mule, and what patterns would a firm see on the sending and the receiving
> side?

## E composition — sufficiency, document by document

**`SYN-TY-02`.** The only eligible document stating the dual-role proposition at
all: §4.2 says coerced first-generation account access means a single customer
may simultaneously be a victim on the sending side and a recruited mule on the
receiving side, and that firms should resist classifying such customers by
whichever role was detected first. It is also the only source of the sending-side
half: coercion as a pattern distinct from deception (§4.1 — no urgency signature,
no payee-novelty signals, a payment pattern inconsistent with the customer's
interests), and why authorisation-based controls cannot separate victim from
participant (§2.1). Remove TY-02 and the question's premise is unsupported.

**`SYN-TY-01`.** The only eligible source of the receiving-side patterns:
coercion of individuals known to the recruiter as a recruitment route, with a
material share of first-generation holders showing indicators of vulnerability,
coercion or deception (§3.1); coaching of the holder's answers, with
inconsistency against account activity itself an indicator (§3.2); the
first-generation lifecycle of quiet period, first material inbound credit
inconsistent with prior activity, rapid onward movement within hours (§5.1);
first-generation disposability (§2.2); many-to-one inbound convergence at the
second generation (§5.2). TY-02 §4.2 asserts the overlap in a single sentence and
points at TY-01 §3 for it. Remove TY-01 and the receiving-side half reduces to an
unfollowed cross-reference.

## Necessity — considered and excluded

**`SYN-CV-01` — MARGINAL, the closest call.** Both E documents cross-reference it
by section for coercion and vulnerability indicators. Excluded because both state
the coercion pattern in their own terms rather than by reference — TY-02 §4.1
gives the coercion signature affirmatively, TY-01 §3.1 names coercion as a
recruitment route — so the answer stands grounded without CV-01, which supplies
indicator taxonomy and customer-treatment expectations. **Stated boundary: if the
query were re-worded to ask which indicators a firm should record, or how such a
customer should be treated, CV-01 would become necessary and E would be three.**
The query wording was chosen deliberately to keep the question typological.

**`SYN-SG-01`.** Control expectations arising from these typologies; the
consequence of the typology, not its grounding. **`SYN-SG-02`.** Reporting
obligations; the query asks nothing about them. **`SYN-TR-01`.** Heavily
cross-referenced but about the effectiveness of detection arrangements, not the
typology being detected. **`SYN-CV-02`.** Post-event redress, upstream of which
the classification question sits. **`OGL-0003`, `OGL-0004`.** Adjacent — a mule
account handles criminal property — but the query is typological, not legal.

## THE RE-AUTHORING TRIGGER — named, not left in prose

Most of the list above is competing content: it would raise retrieval difficulty
at fixed `top_k` without becoming necessary. **`SYN-TY-03` and `SYN-TY-04` are
different in kind.**

Sufficiency is a property of the **eligible set**, not of a document in
isolation. If TY-04 restates mule-network structure and recruitment, then the
claim *"the absence of TY-01 would leave the answer ungrounded"* may simply cease
to be true — and the same for TY-02 against TY-03. Criterion 2 would then fail
**by construction rather than by judgment**, and it could fail in either
direction: the correct E might become three or four documents, or a different
pair entirely.

**If `SYN-TY-03` or `SYN-TY-04` becomes eligible, this scenario is RE-AUTHORED,
not merely re-pinned.** Re-pinning would carry forward an E whose sufficiency
argument no longer holds against the set it is evaluated over. The re-authoring
must itself be a fresh act satisfying the independence criterion — the new E
fixed **before** any retrieval against the new snapshot — because patching E
after seeing results on the wider corpus would destroy the only property that
makes the measurement mean anything.

## Notes carried

The typology identifier is two-level by design: the stem `fraud_typology.` is the
reusable part, so later scenarios over the pending typologies take
`fraud_typology.account_takeover`, `fraud_typology.invoice_redirection` and so
on, and the leaf names the specific typology under test.

**The query wording is load-bearing for E.** It asks what patterns a firm would
*see*, not what indicators it should *record* or how it should *treat* the
customer. That wording is what keeps CV-01 out of E on the sufficiency test. If
the query is ever edited — in particular if "indicators", "vulnerability" or any
treatment or reporting verb enters it — **E must be re-derived, not carried
over**, and CV-01 is the first document to re-test.

Both eligible documents are cross-reference-dense. A retriever may surface the
cited documents at k=5. That is competing content and expected; it means a 0
outcome here should be diagnosed by looking at what displaced the second TY
document, not treated as a bare miss.
