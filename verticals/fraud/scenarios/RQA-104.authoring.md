# RQA-104 — authoring record

Companion to `verticals/fraud/scenarios/RQA-104.yaml`. **Authored 2026-08-17
against the 16-document eligible set at manifest `2026-08-13.8`. E was fixed
here, before any retrieval was run for this scenario.**

## Series and query

Sector Guidance. Two eligible documents: `SYN-SG-01` (4 chunks) and `SYN-SG-02`
(4 chunks), a 1.0x ratio, so no density-stratified note is owed.

> What records must a firm keep of an account opening decision and of a fraud
> suspicion escalated internally, including where the decision was not to act, so
> that the decision can be reconstructed later?

### Why this query, stated because this series is the most exposed

Seven of the fourteen pending documents are Sector Guidance, and their titles map
the detection / intervention / customer-treatment / SAR-quality /
automated-systems / vulnerability / account-exit axes. The query was therefore
built on **the one axis those seven do not occupy** — the evidential record of a
decision, in the two decision classes belonging uniquely to the eligible pair.
Account opening is SG-01's alone; no pending document is about account opening.
The internal escalation route is SG-02's alone, SG-06 being about the quality of
the report that leaves the firm rather than the route inside it. The query says
"escalated internally" for exactly that reason.

## E composition — sufficiency, document by document

**`SYN-SG-01`.** The account-opening limb rests wholly here. §6.1 states the
obligation: firms retain the inputs to each account opening decision in a form
supporting later reconstruction, and where a subsequently confirmed fraud
involves a recently opened account the Authority's first questions concern what
the firm knew at opening. §5.1 adds the second record class the query reaches —
fraud outcomes analysed by onboarding cohort with the resulting control
adjustment evidenced — and §5.2 makes the absence of that analysis a finding in
its own right. No other eligible document states any account-opening record
expectation.

**`SYN-SG-02`.** The escalation limb rests wholly here. §2.3 is the only place in
the eligible corpus enumerating what the designated function must record for
every escalation — the substance of the suspicion, the information relied on, the
decision taken, the decision-maker, the rationale — and it is the only source for
the query's "including where the decision was not to act" limb, which it singles
out as the case most in need of a recorded rationale. §2.1 grounds the prior
obligation of a single documented escalation route; §6.1 the annual board-level
analysis.

## Necessity — considered and excluded

**`SYN-DP-01` — MARGINAL, the closest call.** Both E documents point at it by
name for this exact subject: SG-01 §6.1 says DP-01 describes how such records are
weighed, and SG-02 §2.3 cites DP-01 §3 for why decisions not to act need a
recorded rationale. Excluded on sufficiency: DP-01 describes how the Authority
weighs records, which is a different proposition from what a firm must record.
**Had the query read "how will the Authority treat those records", DP-01 would be
necessary and |E| would be 3.**

**`SYN-TR-01` — MARGINAL.** SG-01 §6.1 grounds its own record obligation by
analogy, "on the same principle applied to monitoring interventions in TR-01
§5.2". TR-01 is the source of the analogy, not of the obligation, and the
decision class it covers is neither of the two the query names.

**`SYN-TY-01`.** Cited four times across E, but describes how networks behave,
not what a firm records. **`SYN-CV-01`.** Applies wherever a customer is involved
in an escalated matter; supplies no record obligation. **`OGL-0004`.** Considered
on adjacency — failure to disclose is adjacent to internal escalation — but it
creates a disclosure offence and does not specify what a firm records.

## Obligation D expiry — a dependency, recorded

The obligation_d_justification in the spec is **contingent and its expiry
condition is known now**: if the inclusion decision lands, the series grows from
two documents to nine, the |E| >= 5 route becomes constructible, and the
justification stops holding. Obligation D would then be re-discharged for this
series by a **graded scenario authored against the enlarged set**, not by
re-citing this one. The pending seven span detection, intervention, customer
treatment, SAR quality, automated systems, vulnerability and account exit — ample
material for a five-document expected set.
