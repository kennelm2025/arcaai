# RQA-105 — authoring record

Companion to `verticals/fraud/scenarios/RQA-105.yaml`. **Authored 2026-08-17
against the 16-document eligible set at manifest `2026-08-13.8`. E was fixed

## Series and query

Thematic Review. Two eligible documents: `SYN-TR-01` (5 chunks) and `SYN-TR-02`
(4 chunks), a 1.25x ratio, so no density-stratified note is owed.

> What did the Authority's thematic reviews find about the calibration of fraud
> transaction-monitoring thresholds and of sanctions screening match thresholds,
> and what should a firm be able to evidence about its threshold choices?

## E composition — sufficiency, document by document

**`SYN-TR-01`.** The only eligible document carrying the fraud-monitoring limb.
§3.2 is the sole source for the finding that velocity rules were universally
deployed but inconsistently tuned, that thresholds were observed set to minimise
alert volume rather than to reflect the velocity characteristics of confirmed
fraud in the firm's own outcome data, and for the evidential expectation itself —
that threshold choices must be shown to derive from analysis rather than
operational convenience. §3.1 supplies the per-rule record that expectation
resolves to; §2.2 the counter-case of thresholds unchanged since deployment.
Remove TR-01 and the answer retains no source for either the fraud-side finding
or the standard of evidence, and TR-02's own calibration passage becomes a
dangling cross-reference to a principle stated nowhere in the retrieved set.

**`SYN-TR-02`.** The only eligible document carrying the screening limb. §3.1 is
the sole source for the findings that fuzzy-match calibration varied more than
list content did, that thresholds set too tight allowed transliteration variants
of designated names to pass unmatched, that thresholds set too loose overwhelmed
the handling team, that **both** extremes are findings rather than only the
loose one, and that screening calibration should be evidenced against test cases.
That test-case form of evidence is a distinct answer element from TR-01's
derive-from-analysis form.

**MARGINAL CASE, recorded because it is a judgment.** It is arguable that TR-02's
calibration finding is an application of a principle TR-01 already states, so
that TR-01 alone grounds "the standard" and TR-02 merely instances it. That
reading was rejected: the query asks about screening match thresholds explicitly,
and the screening-specific findings appear only in TR-02.

## Necessity — considered and excluded

**`SYN-DL-02` — the closest call among eligible documents.** TR-01 §3.4 routes
expectations on the data underpinning detection systems to DL-02, and a
calibration answer plainly has a data dependency. Excluded on sufficiency: the
query asks what the reviews **found** about calibration and what must be
evidenced about threshold choices, and every element of that answer is stated in
TR-01 and TR-02 themselves. DL-02 would extend the answer, not ground it.

**`SYN-SG-02` — a term collision, not a subject overlap.** SG-02 carries a
*reporting* threshold; the query concerns *detection and match* thresholds. Both
series members cross-reference it, so it is a mentioned document in both, but it
grounds nothing the query asks.

**`SYN-DP-01`.** Cited for how intervention and discount records are weighed
after the event; the query does not reach post-event assessment. **`SYN-TY-01`,
`SYN-SG-01`, `SYN-DL-01`, `SYN-CV-01`.** Each cross-referenced by one or both
series members; none carries any part of a calibration answer.

## Notes carried

**Known distractor, deliberately not designed around.** "Threshold" carries two
senses in the eligible corpus — calibration thresholds (TR-01 §3.2, TR-02 §3.1)
and the SG-02 reporting threshold, which both series members cite by name. A
retriever may surface SG-02 on the term alone. The query was **not** reworded to
dodge this, because the collision is a real property of the corpus and steering
around it would make the probe easier than the domain is.

**Cross-reference density.** TR-01 and TR-02 cite SG-01, SG-02, DL-01, DL-02,
DP-01, CV-01 and TY-01 by section. Every one was treated as mention rather than
grounding. If the retrieval configuration ever gives weight to citation edges,
this scenario's non-E documents are exactly what such a mechanism would pull in,
which makes RQA-105 a reasonable place to observe that effect — as an
observation, not as an acceptance criterion.

The series is the least dense in the eligible set, so this probe evidences
retrieval from short documents only and nothing about dense-document behaviour.
That limit is carried inside the Obligation D justification rather than promoted
into a density note the plan does not owe.
