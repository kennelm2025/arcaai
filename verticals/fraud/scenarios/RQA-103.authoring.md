## Series and query

Decision Procedures. Two eligible documents: `SYN-DP-01` (4 chunks) and
`SYN-DP-02` (3 chunks), a 1.33x ratio, so no density-stratified note is owed.

> When a fraud-control failure could lead to formal action, how does the
> Authority decide whether to act against the firm, and what additional test must
> be satisfied before it acts against the responsible senior individual?

## E composition — sufficiency, document by document

**`SYN-DP-01`.** The firm limb is grounded nowhere else. §2 states what is
weighed — contemporaneous records; the control-failure/outcome-event distinction,
including that absence of loss does not establish adequacy; escalation and
reporting conduct as an aggravating or mitigating feature distinct from the
underlying failure. §3 states the stages: finding and representation, a referral
decision by a decision-maker independent of the supervision team that made the
finding, formal action before the committee arrangements, publication naming the
firm. DP-02 does not restate these; it incorporates them by reference at §3.2,
which is a pointer, not grounding. An answer on that pointer alone would assert
the existence of considerations it could not state.

**`SYN-DP-02`.** The additional individual test is grounded only here. §2.1
supplies the attribution question: action against an individual requires more
than a failure at the firm, and the Authority must be satisfied the responsible
individual failed to take the steps reasonable for a person in that position —
what they knew or should have known and did or omitted to do. §2.2 identifies the
records answering it, §2.3 the delegation/abdication limit on reasonable
reliance, §3.1-3.3 the individual-track procedure. DP-01 §1.2 does no more than
record that individual decisions are addressed separately; without DP-02 the
answer to "what additional test" is a signpost, not an answer.

**Both single-document alternatives were tested and neither survives.** Dropping
DP-02 loses the test the query names in terms; dropping DP-01 loses the firm
procedure DP-02 only cross-references. E is exactly two.

## Necessity — considered and excluded

**`SYN-SG-02` — MARGINAL, the strongest candidate.** DP-01 §2.3, §3.1 and DP-02
§3.2 all turn on escalation and reporting conduct and cite SG-02 by section.
Rejected on what the query asks: it asks how the decision is made, and the DP
documents state the decision rule in full on their own face. SG-02 supplies the
substantive obligation whose discharge is weighed, not the weighing. A query
worded to ask what conduct is weighed favourably would pull SG-02 into E.

**`SYN-TR-01`.** Cited three times across the series but always as illustration
of a general standard the DP text states itself. **`SYN-SG-01`.** Cited for the
intervention-record expectation and cohort-level learning; the query does not
reach remediation adequacy. **`SYN-DL-01`.** Cited for board consideration and
published supervisory work — mention. **`SYN-TY-01`, `SYN-TY-02`.** Named at
DP-02 §4.2 only to **disclaim** them: nothing penalises an individual for the
acts of the fraudsters described there. A disclaiming reference is the clearest
possible non-grounding mention. **`OGL-0001`–`OGL-0004`.** Criminal law; the
query concerns supervisory decision procedure.

## Notes carried

Corpus fact affecting difficulty, not obvious from chunk counts: DP-01 §1.2 and
DP-02 §1.1 cross-reference each other explicitly, and DP-02 §3.2 incorporates
DP-01 §2 by reference. A retriever finding either document is reading text that
names the other, which may make this conjunction easier than a two-document
conjunction over unrelated documents. That is a property of the corpus and not a
reason to change the spec, but it should temper how much a green here is taken to
evidence about multi-document retrieval generally.
