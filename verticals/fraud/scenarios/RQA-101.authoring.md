## Series and query

Customer Vulnerability. Two eligible documents: `SYN-CV-01` (4 chunks) and
`SYN-CV-02` (3 chunks), a 1.33x ratio, so no density-stratified note is owed.

> On what grounds may a firm decline or reduce reimbursement to a customer who
> was defrauded into authorising a payment, and which vulnerability and coercion
> indicators visible in the firm's own data must be weighed in that assessment?

## E composition — sufficiency, document by document

**`SYN-CV-02`.** The first limb — the grounds on which a decline is permitted —
exists only here. §3.1 states the single exception (gross negligence in the
authorisation, assessed against what that customer in their circumstances could
reasonably have been expected to recognise) and fixes its height. §2.1 supplies
the default the exception cuts against. §3.2 binds the two limbs: vulnerability
at the time of payment is weighed whether or not the customer raised it, and a
firm holding the indicators in its own data cannot decline on a standard the
customer could not meet. Remove CV-02 and the answer has no legal standard at
all. CV-01 nowhere states the exception; its §5.2 defers to CV-02 by name.

**`SYN-CV-01`.** The second limb — which indicators are weighed — is not stated
in CV-02, which discharges the question by explicit cross-reference and
enumerates nothing itself. The enumeration lives in CV-01 §2.1 and §3.1. CV-01
§1.2 additionally supplies the definition the whole weighing rests on —
vulnerability as a state that may be permanent, temporary or situational, not a
category of person — which is what makes "at the time of the payment" a
meaningful qualifier. Remove CV-01 and the second limb collapses to a pointer at
a document that is not present: ungrounded, not merely thinner.

## Necessity — considered and excluded

**`SYN-TR-01`.** CV-02 §3.3 makes intervention quality bear on the assessment and
grounds the criticised practice in TR-01 §4.3. Excluded because the query asks
what the grounds are and which indicators are weighed, not what a properly
conducted intervention consists of. The query was framed to exclude that limb
rather than to exclude TR-01 — had it asked about intervention quality, TR-01 and
CV-01 §4 would both have entered E and this would be a different scenario.

**`SYN-TY-02` — MARGINAL, and the closest call in the scenario.** *For
inclusion:* CV-02 §1.1 scopes itself to the schemes described in TY-02, and §3.2
makes a substantive assertion about them. *For exclusion, which was taken:* the
query asks for indicators visible in the FIRM's own data, and CV-01 §2.1 and §3.1
supply the firm-side observable form of exactly those patterns. A reader with
CV-01 and CV-02 can answer completely; TY-02 would deepen understanding of the
offender behaviour behind the indicators without being load-bearing. Its absence
makes the answer less rich, not ungrounded, which is the line criterion 2 draws.

**`SYN-DL-01`, `SYN-SG-02`.** Cross-referenced from E on the reporting and
escalation axis, which CV-02 §4.3 expressly holds separate from the reimbursement
decision. **`SYN-SG-01`, `SYN-TY-01`.** Cited at CV-02 §2.2 for the receiving
firm's share of the loss, which the query does not ask about.
