# SG-01 — Sector Guidance: Account Opening Controls for Retail Payment Accounts

**Issued by the Authority · Sector Guidance series · Reference SG-01**

## 1. Status and application

1.1 This guidance applies to relevant firms opening retail payment
accounts. It describes how the Authority interprets the account
opening obligations of the Framework and what it will look for in
supervisory work. It is guidance, not rules: a firm may meet the
underlying obligations by other means, but should expect to explain
how.

1.2 This guidance is concerned with the fraud and financial-crime
dimension of account opening. It does not address prudential,
conduct-of-sales or accessibility matters, which are covered
elsewhere in the Framework.

## 2. The risk this guidance addresses

2.1 Accounts opened with weak entry controls are the raw material of
payment fraud. The typology work at TY-01 describes how organised
networks recruit account holders and route fraud proceeds through
chains of recently opened accounts; the Authority's thematic review of
transaction monitoring (TR-01) found that receiving-side weaknesses
were as significant as sending-side ones in the losses examined.

2.2 The Authority does not expect firms to prevent every bad actor
from opening an account. It expects firms to make account opening
meaningfully harder for bad actors than for genuine customers, to know
which of their onboarding cohorts subsequently generate
disproportionate fraud outcomes, and to adjust.

## 3. Identity, device and intent signals at onboarding

3.1 Identity verification should be calibrated to the account's
capabilities. An account capable of same-day outbound payments at
material value warrants stronger verification than one that cannot
transact until further checks complete. Firms offering instant
capability at onboarding should recognise that they have chosen the
higher-risk configuration and control accordingly.

3.2 Device and session signals available at onboarding — device
reputation, session behaviour, the relationship between claimed
identity and digital footprint — are part of the picture the Framework
expects firms to consider. A firm that collects such signals and does
not use them in its opening decision should be able to say why.

3.3 Stated account purpose is a signal, not a formality. Where an
account's early transaction pattern departs sharply from its stated
purpose, that departure should be visible to the firm's monitoring
(see TR-01 §3) and should inform the account's risk standing.

3.4 Firms should apply particular care where onboarding indicators
suggest the applicant may be acting under the direction of another
person. Indicators of coercion and third-party direction are described
in CV-01 §3; recruitment of account holders by mule networks
frequently presents this way at the point of opening (TY-01 §4).

## 4. The first ninety days

4.1 The period immediately after opening carries elevated risk, and
the Authority expects firms to treat it as such. Newly opened accounts
should carry graduated transaction capabilities, enhanced monitoring
sensitivity, or both, until the firm has observed enough genuine
activity to normalise its view of the account.

4.2 The Authority regards the following pattern as warranting
intervention rather than observation: an account under ninety days
old receiving an inbound payment materially larger than any prior
credit, followed by rapid onward dispersal or cash extraction. The
escalation obligations described in SG-02 apply where the threshold in
that guidance is met.

4.3 Graduation of capabilities should be earned by observed conduct,
not by elapsed time alone. Time-based graduation is trivially gamed by
letting an account age quietly before use; the networks described at
TY-01 §5 do exactly this.

## 5. Cohort learning

5.1 Firms should analyse fraud outcomes by onboarding cohort: the
channel, verification route, product configuration and (where
lawful and relevant) acquisition source of the accounts involved in
confirmed fraud. Where a cohort generates disproportionate outcomes,
the firm should adjust the controls applied to that cohort and should
be able to evidence the adjustment.

5.2 The Authority will ask firms for this analysis in supervisory
work. A firm unable to say which of its onboarding routes its fraud
arrives through has not completed the feedback loop this guidance
expects.

## 6. Records

6.1 Firms should retain the inputs to each account opening decision in
a form that supports later reconstruction, on the same principle
applied to monitoring interventions in TR-01 §5.2. Where a
subsequently confirmed fraud involves a recently opened account, the
Authority's first questions will concern what the firm knew at
opening; the decision procedures at DP-01 describe how such records
are weighed.

**End of SG-01.**
