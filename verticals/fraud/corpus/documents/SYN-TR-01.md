# TR-01 — Thematic Review: Transaction Monitoring Effectiveness in Retail Payment Fraud

**Issued by the Authority · Thematic Review series · Reference TR-01**

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1 This review sets out the Authority's findings from its thematic
assessment of transaction monitoring arrangements at fourteen relevant
firms offering retail payment accounts. Fieldwork examined how firms
detect, triage and act on indicators of payment fraud, with particular
attention to authorised push payment fraud and the movement of funds
through newly opened accounts.

1.2 The review should be read alongside the Authority's sector guidance
on account opening controls (see SG-01 §3) and the typology material on
money mule networks (TY-01). Where this review identifies expectations,
those expectations restate obligations that already apply to relevant
firms under the Framework; nothing in this document creates new rules.

1.3 Findings are reported thematically. No individual firm is named.
Where examples are given they are composites constructed for
illustration.

## 2. Summary of findings

2.1 The Authority found material variation in monitoring effectiveness
that did not correlate with firm size or spend. The strongest
arrangements shared three characteristics: monitoring rules maintained
against a documented threat picture rather than a static rulebook;
alert-handling capacity planned against observed alert volumes; and a
feedback loop from confirmed fraud outcomes into rule calibration.

2.2 The weakest arrangements shared a single characteristic: rules
inherited from system implementation and never revisited. At four
firms, the review team identified monitoring rules whose thresholds had
not changed since deployment, in one case seven years earlier, despite
substantial change in the firm's product set and customer base over
that period.

2.3 Alert backlogs were the most frequently observed weakness. At six
firms, the median time from alert generation to first human review
exceeded five business days for alerts not auto-escalated. The
Authority regards a monitoring alert that is not reviewed until the
funds are unrecoverable as, in substance, no monitoring at all.

## 3. Detection: rule design and calibration

3.1 Firms with effective arrangements documented, for each monitoring
rule, the fraud typology it addresses, the data it consumes, its
current thresholds, and the date and rationale of the last calibration.
The review team was able to trace any given rule to its threat
rationale at these firms without assistance from the rule's author.

3.2 Velocity rules were universally deployed but inconsistently
tuned. The Authority observed thresholds set to minimise alert volume
rather than to reflect the velocity characteristics of confirmed fraud
in the firm's own outcome data. Firms should be able to evidence that
threshold choices derive from analysis, not from operational
convenience.

3.3 New-payee and first-payment indicators were strong discriminators
in every firm's confirmed-fraud population, yet three firms applied no
additional scrutiny to first payments to newly created payees. The
combination of a newly added payee, a payment at or near a customer's
historical maximum, and out-of-pattern session characteristics should
prompt intervention before release of funds, not retrospective review
(see DL-01 for the Authority's expectations following recent losses of
this kind).

3.4 Rules should degrade safely. Where a data feed consumed by a
monitoring rule becomes unavailable, the firm should know within one
business day, and the compensating position should be documented in
advance. At two firms, the review team identified rules that had been
silently inoperative for extended periods following upstream data
changes; neither firm could state when the rules had stopped firing.
The Authority's expectations on the data underpinning detection
systems are consolidated in DL-02.

## 4. Triage and intervention

4.1 Alert triage should be risk-ordered, not first-in-first-out. Firms
operating a single undifferentiated queue consistently showed the
longest delays on the alerts that mattered most.

4.2 Intervention before release of funds is materially more effective
than recovery after it. Firms should maintain the capability to hold a
payment pending customer contact where monitoring indicators warrant
it, and should measure the false-positive cost of doing so honestly
rather than treating every hold as a customer-harm event. The
Authority's decision procedures (DP-01 §2) describe how intervention
records are assessed in the event of a subsequent complaint.

4.3 Customer contact scripts at the point of intervention should
address the scam scenario the indicators suggest, not merely confirm
that the customer initiated the payment. A customer under social
engineering will confirm the payment; the question is whether the firm
gave the customer a realistic opportunity to recognise the situation.
Guidance on identifying coercion and vulnerability indicators during
intervention calls is at CV-01 §4.

## 5. Outcome feedback

5.1 Confirmed fraud outcomes should flow back into rule calibration on
a defined cycle. The Authority observed the strongest results at firms
that reviewed the prior quarter's confirmed-fraud population against
the rules that did and did not fire, and adjusted accordingly, with
the analysis and the resulting changes documented.

5.2 Firms should retain the decision trail for monitoring
interventions — the indicators present, the action taken, and the
outcome — in a form that supports later review. A firm that cannot
reconstruct why a payment was or was not held cannot demonstrate that
its arrangements were effective at the time of the decision.

## 6. Actions for relevant firms

6.1 Relevant firms should assess their arrangements against the
findings in this review and be prepared to evidence that assessment.
The Authority will follow up with a targeted information request to a
sample of firms within twelve months of publication.

6.2 Nothing in this review modifies the reporting obligations
described in the Authority's escalation guidance; suspected fraud
meeting the reporting threshold remains reportable irrespective of
monitoring outcome (see SG-02 §5).

**End of TR-01.**
