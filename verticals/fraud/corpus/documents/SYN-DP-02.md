# DP-02 — Decision Procedures: Individual Accountability in Fraud-Control Failures

**Issued by the Authority · Decision Procedures series · Reference DP-02**

## 1. Purpose and status

1.1 This document describes how the Authority decides whether a
fraud-control failure at a relevant firm gives rise to action against
an individual, and the procedure that follows if so. It accompanies
DP-01, which governs action against firms; the two procedures run in
parallel where a matter involves both.

1.2 The Framework attaches specified responsibilities to named senior
individuals at relevant firms. This document uses "responsible
individual" to mean the person to whom the relevant responsibility
was attached at the material time.

## 2. The attribution question

2.1 Action against an individual requires more than a failure at the
firm. The Authority must be satisfied that the responsible individual
failed to take the steps it was reasonable for a person in that
position to take. The question is not whether fraud occurred, nor
whether the firm's controls failed, but what this individual knew or
should have known, and did or omitted to do.

2.2 The records that answer the attribution question are largely the
same records that answer the firm-level question: board and committee
minutes recording consideration of matters such as the DL-01 letter
(DL-01 §1.2), the appetite articulation expected by DL-01 §3.1,
escalation records under SG-02 §2.3, and the cohort analysis
described in SG-01 §5. An individual who ensured these existed and
acted on what they showed is in a strong position; an individual in
whose area they are absent should expect the absence attributed.

2.3 Delegation is permitted; abdication is not. A responsible
individual may rely on competent delegates and on management
information, but reliance must be reasonable: information that was
implausible on its face, or that a reasonable holder of the role
would have tested (for example, a monitoring function reporting zero
material alert backlog against the volumes discussed in TR-01 §2.3),
does not support reliance merely because it was reported.

## 3. Stages of the procedure

3.1 **Notice.** Where the Authority is minded to pursue an
individual, it gives written notice identifying the responsibility
concerned, the alleged failing, and the material relied on. The
individual has a stated period to respond and may do so separately
from the firm; the Authority provides the individual with the
material even where the firm holds it.

3.2 **Decision to proceed.** A decision-maker independent of the
investigating team decides whether to proceed. The considerations in
DP-01 §2 apply with the attribution question of §2 above added; the
individual's own escalation conduct — whether they raised the matter
through the routes described in SG-02 §2 — is weighed distinctly and
can be decisive in either direction.

3.3 **Formal process and publication.** Formal action follows the
committee arrangements in the Framework. Concluded actions against
individuals are published with reasons. The Authority does not
publish the fact that an individual was investigated and not
pursued.

## 4. Cooperation and candour

4.1 Individuals are expected to cooperate with the procedure and to
be candid. Candour about a failing is weighed in mitigation;
constructed accounts that the contemporaneous record contradicts are
weighed the other way, and are assessed as conduct in their own
right.

4.2 Nothing in this procedure penalises an individual for the acts
of the fraudsters described in TY-01 and TY-02. The Authority's
concern is the discharge of the responsibilities the individual
held, on the standard in §2.1.

**End of DP-02.**
