# TY-06 — Typology Report: Romance and Social Engineering Fraud

**Issued by the Authority · Typology series · Reference TY-06**

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This report describes the grooming-based subset of authorised
push payment fraud: frauds in which the deception is not a single
false invoice or a single urgent call, but a relationship built
over weeks or months for the purpose of extraction. Romance fraud
is the archetype; the same mechanics appear in long-conduct
investment frauds and in frauds on the recently bereaved. The
parent typology is TY-03, and everything there about the mechanics
of authorised payment applies; this report addresses what the long
grooming arc changes.

## 2. The grooming arc

2.1 Reported matters show a consistent arc. Contact is made on a
dating platform, a social network, or through an unsolicited but
warm approach. A period of intensive relationship-building follows
in which no money is mentioned at all: daily contact, escalating
intimacy or trust, and the systematic isolation of the victim from
sceptical friends and family — the fraudster becomes the person the
victim talks to most, and often the person they talk to about the
fraudster's own legitimacy.

2.2 The first request is small and repayable, and frequently is
repaid: a proof of trust that costs the network little and
recalibrates the victim's sense of risk. Requests then escalate in
amount and emotional stakes — the medical emergency, the customs
fee holding a fortune, the investment opportunity shared as a
secret. By the late arc, victims are not so much deceived on each
payment as committed to the relationship in which the payments make
sense; reported matters include victims who continued paying after
being told by their firm, in terms, that they were being defrauded.

2.3 The statutory analysis is unchanged from TY-03: the fabricated
identity, the fabricated emergencies and the fabricated returns are
false representations under section 2 of the Fraud Act 2006 (the
extract at OGL-0001), made dishonestly and intending gain. The arc
merely means the representations number in the thousands and are
distributed across months.

## 3. What the arc changes for detection

3.1 The single-payment pressure indicators at TY-03 §4 are muted
here. Individual payments in a romance fraud can look calm: the
victim is not on a call with a fraudster mid-payment, because the
relationship has already done the work the call does in other APP
variants. Detection weight shifts from the payment moment to the
pattern across payments.

3.2 The pattern-level indicators observed in reported matters
include: a new and escalating series of payments to an individual
payee with no prior history; payments to money transmission
services or cryptoasset platforms from customers with no prior use
of either; payments whose stated purposes rotate through the
classic scripts — travel, medical, customs, investment top-up;
liquidation of savings products or new borrowing feeding the
payment series; and, across the same period, changes in the
customer's own inbound and outbound contact patterns consistent
with the isolation at §2.1.

3.3 Intervention conversations in this typology fail differently
than elsewhere. The victim has been coached — often gently, by the
relationship itself — to expect the firm's questions and to regard
them as intrusions into a private matter. Reported matters show
victims presenting rehearsed, plausible purposes, and firms
accepting them because each individual payment, taken alone,
survived challenge. The design implication is that the challenge
must be built on the series, put to the customer as a pattern, and
handled by staff trained for a conversation in which the customer
may defend the fraudster.

## 4. Vulnerability is the operative fact

4.1 Selection is not accidental. The typology targets loneliness,
recent bereavement, cognitive decline and social isolation, and
then deepens the isolation it finds. A material share of victims in
reported matters meet the characteristics addressed in the customer
vulnerability guidance at CV-02, and firms should treat a suspected
romance fraud as presumptively involving a customer in vulnerable
circumstances: the fraud is evidence of the vulnerability, having
been aimed at it.

4.2 That presumption should shape the whole response — the pace and
setting of intervention conversations, the involvement of trusted
contacts where the customer consents, the handling of repeat
payment attempts after a first intervention, and the after-care
where losses have crystallised. Repeat victimisation is a known
feature of this typology: lists of responsive victims are traded,
and a customer defrauded once will be approached again, sometimes
by a "recovery agent" offering to retrieve the earlier losses for
a fee. The CV series addresses repeat victimisation directly, and
firms should connect their fraud and vulnerability operations so
that the second approach meets a forewarned firm.

## 5. Use of this report

5.1 Firms should test whether their monitoring can see the payment
series at §3.2 as a series, and whether their intervention model
can carry the conversation at §3.3. This report is descriptive;
the scripts rotate, but the arc — build, isolate, extract,
escalate — has been stable across years of reported matters, and
controls anchored on the arc age better than controls anchored on
this season's cover story.
