# DL-01 — Letter to Chief Executives: Expectations Following Recent Authorised Push Payment Fraud Losses

**Issued by the Authority · Chief Executive letter series · Reference DL-01**

Dear Chief Executive,

## 1. Why we are writing

1.1 Losses to authorised push payment fraud reported to the Authority
by relevant firms rose materially in the last reporting year, with the
sharpest increase in payments made from accounts opened within the
preceding ninety days. We are writing to every relevant firm to set
out, ahead of our planned supervisory work, what we expect boards to
have assured themselves of. This letter does not create new
obligations; it draws together expectations that already apply.

1.2 We ask that you table this letter at your next board risk
committee and that the committee minute its consideration. Our
supervisors may ask to see that minute.

## 2. What we have observed

2.1 The fraud pattern is consistent across firms: a customer is
socially engineered into authorising a payment, frequently to a payee
added minutes earlier, frequently at or near the customer's historical
maximum payment value, and frequently following an inbound contact the
firm never sees. Our thematic review of transaction monitoring
(TR-01 §3.3) found that three firms in the review sample applied no
additional scrutiny to exactly this combination of indicators.

2.2 Receiving-side controls matter as much as sending-side controls.
The typology material at TY-01 describes the account networks through
which these funds move; a substantial share of first-generation
receiving accounts in reported cases were opened with the firm
receiving the funds less than ninety days before first use. Account
opening controls are addressed in SG-01, and we do not repeat that
guidance here.

## 3. What we expect

3.1 **Boards own the risk appetite.** We expect your board to have
articulated, in terms capable of being tested, how much fraud loss the
firm is prepared to tolerate in exchange for payment speed and
customer friction, and to review outcomes against that articulation.
A firm that has never declined or held a payment on fraud grounds has
not set an appetite; it has declined to.

3.2 **Intervention capability before funds leave.** We expect firms to
be able to hold a payment that presents the indicator combination
described at 2.1 pending contact with the customer, and to evidence
that the contact addresses the likely scam scenario rather than merely
re-confirming authorisation (TR-01 §4.3). Where your firm cannot
currently do this, we expect a dated plan.

3.3 **Vulnerability is not an edge case.** A material share of victims
in reported cases exhibited indicators of vulnerability that were
visible to the firm before the payment. Our guidance at CV-01 applies
in full, and we draw particular attention to the expectations on
recognising coercion during live customer contact.

3.4 **Report what the Framework requires.** Escalation and reporting
obligations are unchanged by anything in this letter (SG-02 §5).
Firms should not treat recovery of funds as extinguishing a reporting
obligation that had already arisen.

## 4. What we will do

4.1 Our supervisory work in the coming year will test the expectations
in this letter at a sample of firms. Where we find that a board cannot
evidence its consideration of this letter, we will regard that as
itself a finding. The procedures we follow where findings lead to
formal action are set out in DP-01.

4.2 We will publish aggregate observations from that work. We will not
name firms in that publication, though the Authority's enforcement
powers remain available where the facts warrant them.

Yours faithfully,

**Director of Payments Supervision**
*for the Authority*

**End of DL-01.**
