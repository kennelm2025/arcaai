# SG-08 — Sector Guidance: Vulnerable Customers in Fraud Prevention and Intervention

**Issued by the Authority · Sector Guidance series · Reference SG-08**

## 1. Status and application

1.1 This guidance applies to relevant firms in the design,
calibration and operation of fraud controls and of the interventions
that carry them, as those bear on customers who may be vulnerable.
It describes how the Authority interprets the Framework's
obligations in that respect, and what it will look for in
supervisory work. It is guidance, not rules: a firm may meet the
underlying obligations by other means, but should expect to explain
how.

1.2 This guidance is concerned with the period before a fraud is
suspected, and with the interventions a firm makes within it. The
customer-facing response once fraud is suspected is addressed at
SG-05; in particular, the assessment required at SG-05 §4.1 is that
guidance's requirement and is not restated here. This guidance is
likewise not a statement of any firm's obligations in respect of
reimbursement, which are addressed in the applicable scheme rules.

1.3 What the Authority means by vulnerability, and the indicators by
which a firm may recognise it, are set out at CV-01, which records
at §1.2 that vulnerability is a state rather than a category of
person and may be permanent, temporary or situational. The payment
journey and the controls operating across it are addressed at SG-04,
which at §4.3 leaves the treatment of vulnerability in firms' fraud
controls to be addressed elsewhere in the Framework. This guidance
is that treatment, and assumes both.

## 2. The risk this guidance addresses

2.1 Fraud controls are calibrated, and calibration proceeds from a
picture of how customers ordinarily behave. SG-04 §4.3 records the
consequence for the payment journey: calibration against a single
behavioural norm will underweight exactly the customers the typology
identifies. The Authority's concern in this guidance is that the
same consequence runs through a firm's fraud controls generally, and
not only through its journey. A control set to the centre of a
population is at its weakest at that population's edges, which is
where susceptibility is greatest. It does not follow that the
customers concerned can be carved out and handled under a separate
arrangement. On CV-01 §1.2 vulnerability is a state a customer moves
into and out of, so the customer who needs the firm's protection
today may have been unremarkable in its data last month; controls
that depend on a standing list of customers to protect will protect
the customers who were on the list.

2.2 The customer who most needs an intervention is often the least
able to use it. SG-04 §3.4 records that a customer may be coached
through the firm's questions and warnings by a fraudster present in
the interaction, and CV-01 §3.1 describes what that presence looks
like in the firm's own contact: rehearsed answers inconsistent with
the account's history, instructions the customer visibly reads, an
inability to explain a transaction in the customer's own words. An
intervention resting on the customer's unaided judgement is weakest
in precisely the cases this guidance concerns.

2.3 The Authority's thematic review of payment journey controls at
TR-03 examines firms' practice in this area; this guidance sets out
the expectations against which that practice will be assessed, and
the two should be read together.

## 3. Vulnerability in the design and calibration of controls

3.1 A firm should be able to say how its controls perform for the
customers its own data marks. The indicators at CV-01 §2.1 — a
marked change in transaction pattern following a life event visible
to the firm, communications indicating confusion about products
previously well understood, contact and payment activity suggesting
live direction — are drawn from data the firm already holds for
other purposes. A firm that holds such signals and does not make
them available to the controls deciding when and how to intervene
should expect to be asked why.

3.2 CV-01 §2.2 expects a firm that notices heightened susceptibility
to adjust its handling: greater monitoring sensitivity, lower
intervention thresholds, and routing to staff equipped for the
conversation. The Authority's expectation in supervisory work is
evidential. A firm should be able to show that the adjustment exists
as an operating difference rather than as a policy statement — which
customers it reaches, what it changes, at what threshold it engages,
when it was last reviewed, and on what evidence.

3.3 Classifications taken for operational convenience can withdraw
protection. CV-01 §3.3 records that a coerced customer may be at
once a fraud victim and a recruited account holder, and that the
firm's first classification should not foreclose the other. Where a
firm's controls route a customer classified on the receiving side
out of the treatment this guidance describes, the classification has
not merely labelled the customer; it has removed the protection.
Firms should be able to identify the customers their own
classifications have moved, and to satisfy themselves that each
movement was warranted.

3.4 Recording a customer as vulnerable is not itself a protective
act. The protection is the changed handling, and a marker that
alters nothing downstream of itself is a record of a judgement the
firm did not act on. Firms should be able to demonstrate what
follows from such a marker in their systems — what it changes, for
whom, and at what point in the interaction — and should treat the
absence of any demonstrable consequence as a finding about the
control rather than as a documentation gap.

## 4. Intervention where vulnerability is indicated

4.1 SG-04 §3.2 expects friction to be proportionate to indication
rather than uniform across the journey. Indication for that purpose
includes indication of vulnerability. Where the signals at CV-01
§2.1 are present, the case for a stronger intervention is stronger,
and a firm whose intervention strength varies only with payment
value or payee novelty has not given effect to that expectation.

4.2 The design of the intervention matters more here than at any
other point in the journey. SG-04 §3.4 records that interventions
asking a customer to confirm their own uncoerced intent are
systematically defeated by live coaching. Where the indicators at
CV-01 §3.1 are also present, such an intervention is not merely weak
but adverse to the customer's interests, since the confirmation it
obtains will be read afterwards as evidence that the customer was
content. Firms should prefer interventions that interrupt the
direction — a delay the directing party cannot sustain, an outbound
contact on a channel that party does not control — and should put
questions of the kind CV-01 §4.1 describes, which a coached customer
finds hard to answer with a supplied line.

4.3 CV-01 §4.2 records that staff should be permitted, and should
know they are permitted, to extend a hold where the conversation
deepens rather than resolves concern. The Authority will look for
evidence that the permission is real. Firms should be able to show
that extensions occur, in what proportion of cases and on whose
authority, and should examine an arrangement in which they never do:
an intervention that always concludes within its allotted time is
being ended by the clock rather than by its purpose.

4.4 Where an intervention does not resolve concern and a fraud comes
to be suspected, the firm's obligations under SG-05 arise and the
assessment required at SG-05 §4.1 falls to be performed as part of
the response that guidance describes. Where instead the customer
proceeds after a properly conducted intervention, the substance of
the conversation should be recorded in the form described at CV-01
§4.3. Neither outcome is a closure: the first begins a response, and
the second leaves the firm holding a customer it has already
identified as at risk.

## 5. Outcomes and onward expectations

5.1 Firms should analyse confirmed fraud losses against the
indicators they held before the loss. The principle is that applied
to journey cohorts at SG-04 §5.1, directed at the subject of this
guidance: where losses concentrate among customers whose
susceptibility the firm's own data had already marked, the finding
concerns the firm's controls and not its customers. A firm unable to
perform that analysis cannot know whether the adjustments described
at §3.2 are working.

5.2 Further expectations of the Authority bearing on the matters
addressed in this guidance are set out in the Customer Vulnerability
series at CV-05, which firms should read together with this
guidance.

## 6. Records

6.1 Firms should retain, for each material intervention in scope of
this guidance, the indicators present including any bearing on
vulnerability, the adjustment to handling applied, the substance of
any conversation with the customer, the disposition and its basis,
and the identity of any person who exercised judgement — in a form
that supports later reconstruction, on the principle applied at
SG-04 §6.1. The record of the assessment performed under SG-05 is
governed by SG-05 §6.2 and is not duplicated by this paragraph.

6.2 A firm should be able to answer, for any intervention, what its
data showed about the customer, what its controls did differently in
consequence, and why the firm acted as it did. Where a confirmed
fraud follows an intervention involving a customer the firm's own
indicators had marked, those are the Authority's first questions.

**End of SG-08.**
