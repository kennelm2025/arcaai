# SG-02 — Sector Guidance: Internal Escalation and Reporting of Suspected Fraud

**Issued by the Authority · Sector Guidance series · Reference SG-02**

## 1. Status and application

1.1 This guidance applies to relevant firms and describes the
Authority's expectations for the internal escalation of suspected
fraud and for reports made to the Authority under the Framework. It
should be read with the account opening guidance at SG-01 and the
monitoring findings at TR-01; the vulnerability guidance at CV-01
applies wherever a customer is involved in an escalated matter.

## 2. Internal escalation

2.1 Every relevant firm should operate a single, documented route by
which any member of staff can escalate a suspicion of fraud, and
should be able to show that the route is known and used. The Authority
has observed firms with elaborate escalation frameworks and no
escalations from customer-facing staff in a reporting year; it treats
that combination as a finding about the framework, not about the
absence of fraud.

2.2 Escalation should not be filtered by seniority. A suspicion
raised by junior staff should reach the designated function on the
same footing as one raised by a manager. Where a firm's process
requires line-management endorsement before an escalation proceeds,
the Authority will ask what happens when the concern involves the
line manager.

2.3 The designated function should record, for every escalation
received: the substance of the suspicion, the information relied on,
the decision taken, the decision-maker, and the rationale — including
for decisions not to act. Decisions not to act are the ones most in
need of a recorded rationale, for the reasons set out in DP-01 §3.

## 3. The reporting threshold

3.1 A report to the Authority is required where a relevant firm knows
or suspects, or has reasonable grounds to know or suspect, that
conduct within the Framework's fraud provisions has occurred and the
firm's connection to it meets the conditions in this section. The
test has an objective limb: a firm cannot avoid the obligation by
declining to form a suspicion that the information available to it
would support.

3.2 Suspicion does not require certainty, quantified loss, or an
identified perpetrator. It requires more than speculation: a
possibility that is more than fanciful, grounded in something the
firm has seen. The Authority does not expect firms to resolve the
question of guilt before reporting; that is not their function.

3.3 The obligation arises when the threshold is met, not when
investigation concludes. Internal investigation may proceed in
parallel with reporting; it is not a reason to defer it. Recovery of
funds does not extinguish an obligation that has already arisen
(DL-01 §3.4 restates this in the context of push payment fraud).

## 4. Timing, content and quality of reports

4.1 Reports should be made promptly once the threshold is met. The
Authority recognises that "promptly" is judged in context; it will
not accept queue management as context. A report delayed because the
reporting team was under-resourced is a late report.

4.2 A report should convey what the firm actually knows and how it
knows it: the accounts and transactions concerned, the indicators
observed, the customers or staff involved so far as known, and what
the firm has done. Reports drafted to minimise the firm's apparent
exposure are counterproductive and are noticed.

4.3 Where the matter involves an account network of the kind
described in TY-01, the report should say so and should identify the
connected accounts the firm can see. Fragmentary reports that treat
each account in a visible network as an isolated matter degrade the
value of reporting for every participant in the system.

## 5. Interaction with customer contact and tipping off

5.1 Firms should design intervention and customer-contact practices
(TR-01 §4.3) so that legitimate contact with a customer about a
payment does not disclose the existence or content of a report. Staff
conducting such contact should know what they may and may not say,
and the firm should be able to evidence the training.

5.2 Nothing in this guidance prevents a firm from declining or
delaying a transaction while a reporting decision is made, and the
Authority expects firms to use that latitude where funds are at
imminent risk (SG-01 §4.2 describes one pattern where this is
expected).

## 6. Governance

6.1 The board should receive, at least annually, an analysis of the
firm's escalation and reporting activity: volumes, sources,
decisions, timeliness, and the outcomes of reported matters so far as
known. The decision procedures at DP-01 describe how the Authority
weighs board engagement when assessing a firm's arrangements after a
failure.

**End of SG-02.**
