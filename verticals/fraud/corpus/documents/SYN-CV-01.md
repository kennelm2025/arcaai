# CV-01 — Guidance: Customer Vulnerability in Fraud Prevention and Response

**Issued by the Authority · Customer Vulnerability series · Reference CV-01**

## 1. Status and application

1.1 This guidance applies to relevant firms in the design and
operation of fraud controls and in their response to customers
affected by fraud. It sits alongside the monitoring, onboarding and
escalation guidance (TR-01, SG-01, SG-02) and is cross-referenced by
the Authority's letter on push payment fraud (DL-01 §3.3).

1.2 The Authority uses "vulnerability" to mean any characteristic or
circumstance that increases a customer's susceptibility to fraud or
reduces their capacity to recover from it. Vulnerability is a state,
not a category of person: it may be permanent, temporary or
situational, and a customer may move in and out of it.

## 2. Recognising heightened susceptibility

2.1 Indicators visible to a firm in ordinary data include: marked
change in transaction pattern following a life event visible to the
firm; increasing frequency of payments to a single new counterparty
(the cultivation signature of TY-02 §3.3); repeated near-simultaneous
contact and payment activity suggesting live direction; and customer
communications indicating confusion about products or transactions
previously well understood.

2.2 A firm is not expected to diagnose its customers. It is expected
to notice what its own data shows and to adjust its handling: greater
monitoring sensitivity, lower intervention thresholds, and routing to
staff equipped for the conversation.

## 3. Coercion and third-party direction

3.1 Indicators that a customer may be acting under another person's
direction include: scripted or rehearsed answers, particularly where
inconsistent with the account's history (TY-01 §3.2); a third party
present, audible or messaging during contact; reluctance or inability
to explain a transaction in the customer's own words; instructions
that the customer visibly reads; and requests to increase limits or
capabilities that the customer cannot connect to any need of their
own.

3.2 These indicators arise at onboarding (SG-01 §3.4), during
intervention calls (TR-01 §4.3) and in branch. Staff should be
trained to slow the interaction, separate the customer from the
directing party where possible, and escalate through the SG-02 §2
route rather than resolving the matter at the counter.

3.3 A coerced customer may be simultaneously a fraud victim and a
recruited account holder; TY-02 §4.2 addresses this overlap. The
firm's first classification should not foreclose the other.

## 4. Intervention conversations

4.1 The purpose of an intervention conversation with a potentially
vulnerable customer is to give them a genuine opportunity to
recognise their situation, not to obtain a confirmation that
transfers responsibility. Scripts should describe the scam scenario
the indicators suggest, in concrete terms, and should ask questions a
coached customer finds hard to answer with a supplied line.

4.2 Staff should be permitted, and should know they are permitted,
to extend a hold where the conversation deepens rather than resolves
concern. A conversation ended by the clock is not an intervention.

4.3 Where the customer proceeds after a properly conducted
intervention, the firm should record the conversation's substance in
the form described in TR-01 §5.2. That record protects the customer
and the firm alike, and is central to how the matter is later
assessed under DP-01 §2.1.

## 5. After the fraud

5.1 A customer who has been defrauded is, at that moment, within
this guidance almost by definition. Firms should ensure that
recovery, reimbursement assessment and reporting processes do not
require the customer to repeatedly re-narrate the events to
successive teams, and that staff handling the case can recognise
distress and respond to it.

5.2 Reimbursement assessment should weigh vulnerability at the time
of the payment, on the firm's own indicators, not solely on what the
customer can now articulate; CV-02 sets out the reimbursement and
redress expectations in full. The escalation and reporting obligations
of SG-02 apply irrespective of the reimbursement outcome.

## 6. Governance

6.1 Boards should satisfy themselves that vulnerability handling is
embedded in fraud operations rather than maintained as a parallel
policy, and should see outcome analysis distinguishing vulnerable
customers within the reporting described in SG-02 §6.1.

**End of CV-01.**
