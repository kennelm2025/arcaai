# SG-05 — Sector Guidance: Customer Treatment Following Suspected Fraud

**Issued by the Authority · Sector Guidance series · Reference SG-05**

## 1. Status and application

1.1 This guidance applies to relevant firms from the point at which
a fraud affecting a customer is suspected — whether surfaced by the
firm's own controls or reported by the customer — until the firm's
response is complete. It describes how the Authority interprets the
Framework's obligations as they bear on the treatment of that
customer. It is guidance, not rules, save where expressly stated
otherwise: a firm may meet the underlying obligations by other
means, but should expect to explain how.

1.2 This guidance is concerned with the customer-facing response to
suspected fraud. Intervention in payments before release, the
monitoring of accounts in operation, and firms' obligations in
respect of reimbursement are addressed elsewhere in the Framework
and in the applicable scheme rules. The internal escalation and
reporting of suspicions is addressed in SG-02; this guidance governs
what the firm does for the customer while those obligations run
their course.

## 2. The risk this guidance addresses

2.1 A customer defrauded once can be harmed twice: first by the
fraud, then by a response that is slow, disbelieving, or indifferent
to their circumstances. The Authority regards the quality of a
firm's response as part of its fraud controls, not an aftercare
courtesy. The typology work at TY-03 records where the money goes
and how quickly (TY-03 §3); the period immediately following
suspicion is simultaneously the recovery window for funds, the
period of greatest distress for the customer, and — in a material
fraction of cases — a period in which the deception is still live.

2.2 The last point deserves emphasis. The pretexts described at
TY-03 §2 do not always end at the payment: the customer may still be
in contact with the fraudster, still persuaded by the pretext, and
still acting on instruction when the firm first makes contact. A
response designed on the assumption that the customer already knows
they have been defrauded will fail against the cases where they do
not.

## 3. The response process

3.1 The Authority expects firms to operate a defined response
process for suspected fraud, with named steps, defined timeframes,
and ownership held by named individuals — accountable ownership, not
a departmental address. A response assembled case by case, dependent
on which colleague receives the report, is not a process; it is an
accident of rostering.

3.2 The first steps concern the money and the channel. On suspicion,
the firm should act to secure the account and any remaining funds,
and to prevent further loss, before or in parallel with any other
step. First contact with the customer should be made on a channel
the firm has independent reason to trust: a channel previously and
independently verified, a channel the firm itself initiates, or one
established through a separate authentication event — and not a
channel the customer has been using under the suspected deception.
The reason is recorded at TY-03 §2: the fraudster may be present in,
or in control of, the channels through which the customer ordinarily
transacts, and a response that reaches the customer only through a
compromised channel briefs the fraudster on the firm's suspicion.

3.3 Contact with the customer should be designed with the escalation
and reporting obligations of SG-02 in mind. Escalation proceeds per
SG-02 §2 on its own clock; nothing in this guidance delays it. What
the firm may say to the customer, and when, is constrained by the
interaction described at SG-02 §5, and the response process should
specify how customer communication is conducted within that
constraint rather than leaving each handler to improvise the
boundary.

3.4 The customer's own account of events is evidence, and taking it
is part of the response. It should be taken once, well, by someone
equipped to take it — not repeatedly, across departments, by staff
who each start from nothing. Each retelling costs the customer and
degrades the account.

3.5 Timing matters as much as content. A response measured in days
reaches a customer whose funds have already moved on (TY-03 §3) and
who may have been contacted again by the fraudster in the interval.
The Authority expects the first substantive contact on the same
working day as the suspicion arises, except where doing so would
compromise the security of the account or the integrity of the
escalation under SG-02.

## 4. The vulnerability assessment

4.1 The response process must include an assessment of whether the
affected customer is vulnerable. This is a required step, not a
matter of good practice: it is to be performed in every case in
scope of this guidance, and its outcome recorded, before the firm
settles the pace, channel and content of its further dealings with
the customer.

4.2 The criteria and indicators for that assessment are those set
out in the Authority's work on customer vulnerability at CV-03. The
assessment is not a segmentation exercise: its purpose is to
establish what this customer, in these circumstances, needs from the
response — the pace and channel of contact, the support offered, any
referral made, and the manner in which the customer's account of
events is taken.

4.3 The typology work at TY-03 §5 records that this fraud class
selects for vulnerability: the customers most likely to be in scope
of this guidance are disproportionately those for whom the
assessment will alter the response. A firm that treats the
assessment as a formality discharged by default answers has not
performed it.

## 5. Outcomes and onward decisions

5.1 The response record feeds the firm's onward decision-making in
respect of the incident. The decision procedures at DP-04 describe
how such records are weighed in the decisions that follow a fraud;
the response process should produce a record capable of supporting
them: the firm's assessment of what occurred, the actions taken and
their timing, and the customer's engagement with the response. A
record that consists of a closure code, without the basis for it,
cannot support the decisions DP-04 describes.

5.2 Where the incident reveals that a fraud-control failure
contributed to the loss, questions of individual accountability
arise, and the Authority's approach to attributing responsibility is
set out at DP-02 §2. Firms should conduct and document the response
on the footing that the candour expectations described at DP-02 §4
apply to the response record as they apply to any other account of a
control failure.

## 6. Records

6.1 Firms should retain, for each incident in scope of this
guidance, a record supporting later reconstruction of the response:
the point of suspicion, the steps taken and their timing, the
channels used for contact, the customer's account as taken, the
vulnerability assessment performed under §4 and its outcome, and the
basis of each material decision. The Authority's first questions of
a firm whose response is challenged will concern what the process
required, what was done, and when.

6.2 The record of the vulnerability assessment is not ancillary. A
firm unable to show, for a given incident, that the assessment
required by §4.1 was performed and acted on has, in the Authority's
view, failed to operate the process this guidance describes.

**End of SG-05.**
